## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest route? This visualization showcases **Bidirectional Dijkstra's Algorithm**, a sophisticated approach to solving the shortest path problem in weighted graphs. Unlike a standard Dijkstra search that expands outwards from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one from the **start node** and another from the **destination node**. These searches expand outwards, exploring the graph and prioritizing neighbors based on their accumulated cost from their respective starting points. At its core, Bidirectional Dijkstra employs **priority queues** (often implemented using min-heaps) to efficiently manage which node to explore next, always selecting the node with the smallest tentative distance from its origin. This dual-pronged exploration significantly prunes the search space, drastically reducing the number of nodes that need to be visited.

This isn't just theoretical; we're bringing it to life with a **Real Map Implementation** of Lisbon's historic Belém district! Here, each **intersection** represents a **node** in our graph, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**, each with a weight reflecting travel time or distance. As you observe the visualization, the **red and green lines** illustrate the expansion of our two searches – the red from the start, the green from the end. The moment these search frontiers meet, our algorithm has successfully identified the shortest path. The **yellow line** then highlights this optimal route. It's crucial to note that real-world road networks are far more complex than simple grids. The irregular nature of streets, varying traffic patterns, and one-way systems introduce significant challenges that Bidirectional Dijkstra, with its optimized exploration, is uniquely positioned to handle efficiently.
