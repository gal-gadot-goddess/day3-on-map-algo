Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest route through a sprawling city? This visualization showcases **Bidirectional Dijkstra**, a sophisticated algorithm designed for ultimate optimization. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from your starting point (Prague's Žižkov district, in this case!) and one backward from your destination. Both searches use **priority queues** to efficiently explore their respective graphs, always prioritizing the nodes with the smallest known distance from their respective starting points. This parallel exploration significantly prunes the search space, as the searches only need to meet in the "middle" to guarantee the globally shortest path. The algorithm intelligently decides which search to expand next, often by comparing the frontier sizes of the two searches, aiming to minimize the total work done.

Unlike abstract grids, real-world road networks exhibit non-uniform edge weights and complex connectivity, making Bidirectional Dijkstra's optimized approach particularly impactful in achieving faster pathfinding.
