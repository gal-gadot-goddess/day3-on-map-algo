**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how a navigation app might *quickly* suggest a route, even if it's not always the absolute shortest? Meet the **Greedy Best-First Search algorithm**! At its core, this algorithm prioritizes exploring nodes that appear to be closest to the target, based on a heuristic evaluation. It uses a **priority queue** to manage its frontier of nodes to explore. Unlike algorithms that systematically explore all possibilities or consider path cost (like Dijkstra's), Greedy Best-First makes a "greedy" decision at each step, always picking the neighbor that seems most promising *right now* to reach the goal. This makes it incredibly fast, but be warned: it doesn't guarantee optimality and can sometimes get stuck on suboptimal paths or miss the true shortest route altogether!

This visualization showcases the Greedy Best-First algorithm navigating the streets of **Berlin, Mitte**, transforming real-world road networks into a **graph data structure**. Each intersection is a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges**. The vibrant red and green lines you see illustrate the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores outward, attempting to find the target. The bright **yellow line** represents the computed path it eventually settles on. Implementing graph algorithms on complex, real-world road networks introduces unique challenges compared to simpler grid-based scenarios. The number of potential routes and the irregular "shape" of the graph can significantly impact performance and the effectiveness of heuristic choices, showcasing the dynamic interplay between algorithmic logic and messy, real-world data.
