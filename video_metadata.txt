**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how pathfinding algorithms tackle complex real-world environments? In this visualization, we're showcasing the **Greedy Best-First Search** algorithm in action on a real map of London's vibrant Soho district! 🇬🇧

At its core, Greedy Best-First Search is a **heuristic search algorithm**. It doesn't look ahead multiple steps or explore all possible paths. Instead, it prioritizes expanding nodes that appear to be closest to the target, based solely on a **heuristic function**. For this visualization, we're using the **Manhattan distance** (or Euclidean distance, depending on implementation choice, but the principle remains) as our heuristic, estimating the straight-line distance from a current intersection (node) to our destination. It uses a **priority queue** to manage its "to-visit" frontier, always extracting the node with the lowest heuristic value. This makes it incredibly fast and efficient for quickly finding *a* path, though not necessarily the *optimal* one, as it can be easily "tricked" into suboptimal routes if the heuristic is misleading.

Transforming a bustling city like London into a traversable graph is a fascinating application of **Graph Theory**. Each intersection in Soho becomes a **node**, and the streets connecting them are the **edges**. For our visualization:
🔴 The **red lines** represent the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, expanding its search outward from the starting point.
🟢 The **green lines** indicate the **edges that have been considered but not chosen** to expand further at that moment, demonstrating the greedy nature of the algorithm.
💛 The **yellow line** vividly illustrates the **computed path** found by Greedy Best-First Search.

It's important to note the complexities inherent in real-world road networks versus simpler grid-based scenarios. Traffic, one-way streets, road closures, and varying street widths (which influence travel time) add layers of complexity not always captured by a basic graph representation. While this visualization simplifies these factors, it highlights the fundamental challenges and the direct applicability of graph algorithms to navigating our urban landscapes.
