**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Witness the power of optimized pathfinding! Our visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, a remarkable algorithm designed for ultimate efficiency in finding the guaranteed shortest path. Unlike standard Dijkstra, which expands outwards from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra simultaneously launches two searches: one forward from the starting node and one backward from the destination. These searches expand outwards, exploring neighbors based on their cumulative path cost. The core data structure employed here is a **priority queue**, which meticulously orders nodes to be visited by their current shortest known distance from their respective starting points. This ensures that at each step, the algorithm intelligently prioritizes exploring the most promising directions, effectively "meeting in the middle."

This isn't just theoretical! We've translated the abstract concepts of graph theory into a tangible, real-world application using Reykjavik's Miðborg district as our canvas. Here, each **intersection** acts as a **node** in our graph, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**, each imbued with a weight representing travel time or distance. The vibrant red and green lines you see visually represent the "visited edges" – the explored portions of the graph by our forward and backward searches, respectively. The elegant yellow line beautifully illustrates the computed shortest path, a testament to the algorithm's successful convergence. It's crucial to note that real-world road networks present a far more complex and irregular graph structure compared to simple explicit grids. This inherent complexity makes algorithms like Bidirectional Dijkstra, with their intelligent pruning and dual-pronged exploration, significantly more performant than their single-source counterparts in practical scenarios.
