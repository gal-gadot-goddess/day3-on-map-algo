**Real Map Implementation of DFS 🗺️🚀**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization brings **Depth-First Search (DFS)** to life on the dynamic streets of Lagos, specifically showcasing Yaba and Lagos Island!

At its core, DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. It prioritizes depth over breadth. When faced with multiple unvisited neighbors from a current node (intersection), DFS will pick *one* and fully explore its subtree before returning to consider the next unvisited neighbor of the original node. This recursive or stack-based approach means it dives deep down a single path until it hits a dead end or an already visited node, at which point it "backtracks" to the last junction with an unexplored path. For implementation, DFS typically uses a **stack** (either implicitly through recursion or explicitly with a `Stack` data structure) to keep track of the nodes to visit. It's crucial to distinguish this from Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a **queue** and explores layer by layer.

This isn't your typical grid-based maze! We've transformed the intricate urban fabric of Lagos into a graph representation. Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them are the edges**. The vibrant red and green lines you see illustrate the "visited edges" as our DFS algorithm actively explores the city's network. The glowing yellow line signifies the computed optimal path from start to finish. Unlike simple grids where all edges might have uniform weight, real-world road networks have varying complexities: traffic congestion, one-way streets, speed limits, and varying road conditions can all impact the "cost" of traversing an edge. This adds a significant layer of complexity compared to theoretical graph problems, where algorithms like Dijkstra's (often preferred for finding shortest paths in weighted graphs) might be more directly applicable if we were modeling travel time, but DFS here brilliantly demonstrates the concept of exploration and pathfinding within a real-world context.
