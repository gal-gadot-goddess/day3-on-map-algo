**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the fastest route through complex city grids? This visualization showcases the Bidirectional A* algorithm, a true "speed king" in pathfinding. Unlike traditional A*, Bidirectional A* initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the start node and one backward from the goal node. These searches meet in the middle, dramatically reducing the explored search space. At each step, both forward and backward searches prioritize neighbors using a heuristic function ($h(n)$) which estimates the cost from the current node to the goal. This heuristic, often Euclidean or Manhattan distance on a grid, guides the search. Both searches maintain their own open and closed sets. The open set is typically implemented as a **priority queue**, ensuring that nodes with the lowest f-score (g-score + h-score) are explored first. The closed set, often a **hash set**, efficiently stores visited nodes to prevent redundant exploration.

Witnessing this algorithm in action on a real map of Sapporo's vibrant Susukino district brings graph theory to life. We transform this urban landscape into a graph where **nodes represent intersections** and **edges represent the streets connecting them**. The red and green lines you see are the "visited edges" of our dual searches – red marking the forward exploration and green the backward. The moment these two searches "touch" and the algorithm identifies a meeting point, the path is finalized, represented by the bright yellow line. This real-world implementation highlights a key difference from simple grid-based scenarios: real road networks exhibit varying edge weights (traffic, one-way streets, speed limits), making the heuristic more complex but the algorithm's efficiency gains even more pronounced. The number of nodes and edges explored is significantly less than a single unidirectional search, demonstrating Bidirectional A*'s superior performance.
