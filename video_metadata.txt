**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Witness the speed king in action! Our latest visualization showcases the **Bidirectional A*** algorithm, a powerful pathfinding technique that dramatically accelerates route computation. Unlike a standard A* search that expands outwards from a single start point, Bidirectional A* simultaneously runs two searches: one forward from the start and one backward from the goal. Each search maintains its own open list (typically a **priority queue**) ordered by the heuristic cost (estimated distance to the goal) plus the actual cost incurred so far. The searches stop when they meet, significantly reducing the search space compared to a single unidirectional search. This dual-pronged approach leverages an informed heuristic to prioritize promising neighbors in both directions, making it incredibly efficient, especially for large, complex graphs like road networks.

Transforming Quebec City's Saint-Roch district into a navigable graph is a fascinating application of **Graph Theory**. Each **intersection** becomes a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them represent **edges**. In this visualization, the **red and green lines** illustrate the expansion of our two A* searches, signifying the "visited edges" as the algorithm explores the road network. The **yellow line** ultimately reveals the optimal path calculated by the algorithm. While grids offer a predictable structure for pathfinding, real-world road networks present unique challenges. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and complex intersections create a more intricate graph topology. Bidirectional A*'s ability to efficiently prune search space makes it a formidable tool for tackling these real-world complexities, offering a substantial performance advantage over single-direction searches in applications like GPS navigation and logistics.
