**Real Map Implementation of DFS 📍**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization brings Depth-First Search (DFS) to life on the streets of Montreal! 🇨🇦 DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. It prioritizes visiting one unvisited neighbor before exploring others, essentially "plunging deep." This systematic exploration is typically implemented using a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the nodes to visit. When DFS hits a dead end or a fully explored path, it pops the last node from the stack and continues exploring another branch from the most recently reached intersection. This strategy can be incredibly efficient for finding paths or exploring connected components, though it might not always find the *shortest* path in an unweighted graph, and can sometimes get "lost" in very deep branches before finding a solution if not carefully guided.

We've transformed the charming streets of Montreal's Plateau-Mont-Royal into a graph! 🗺️ Each intersection becomes a **node**, and the streets connecting them are the **edges**. In this visualization, the red and green lines represent the **visited edges** as DFS explores the graph. Think of it as the algorithm physically traversing streets, marking its path as it goes. The bright yellow line showcases the **computed path** – the sequence of streets DFS determined to reach a specific destination. While a grid-based graph offers predictable connections, real-world road networks introduce complexities like one-way streets, varying road lengths, and unique intersection geometries. These factors can significantly influence traversal efficiency and the nature of the graph, making real-world graph theory applications both fascinating and challenging.
