**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Witness the speed king in action! Our latest visualization showcases the **Bidirectional A* algorithm**, a powerful pathfinding technique that excels in real-world applications. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional A* initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the start node and another backward from the goal node. These intelligent agents explore the graph, prioritizing neighbors based on a heuristic function combined with the cost to reach them.

Technically, both searches maintain their own open and closed sets. The open set, typically a **priority queue**, is crucial for efficiently retrieving the node with the lowest estimated total cost (f = g + h), where 'g' is the actual cost from the source and 'h' is the heuristic estimate to the goal. When the two searches "meet" in the middle – meaning a node explored by the forward search is found in the backward search's closed set (or vice-versa) – a potential path is identified. The algorithm then meticulously reconstructs the optimal path by merging the segments from both searches, significantly reducing the search space and computational overhead.

Our "Real Map Implementation" brings this abstract concept to life on the bustling streets of **London's Soho**. That's the computed, optimal path from your starting point to your destination. Unlike a rigid grid, real-world road networks present unique challenges. Edge weights (street lengths, traffic conditions) are dynamic and non-uniform, and the graph's topology can be far more complex, making efficient algorithms like Bidirectional A* indispensable for navigating such intricate landscapes.
