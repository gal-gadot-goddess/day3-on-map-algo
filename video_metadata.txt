**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization demonstrates Depth-First Search (DFS) in action, mapping the streets of Helsinki's vibrant Kallio district! 🌍

At its core, DFS is an algorithm for traversing or searching tree or graph data structures. It prioritizes exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When presented with multiple adjacent nodes (intersections in our map), DFS arbitrarily picks one and dives deep. This "greedy" exploration continues until it reaches a dead end or an already visited node. To manage this, DFS typically utilizes a **stack** (explicitly or implicitly via recursion) to keep track of the path it's currently exploring and the nodes it needs to revisit. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS's stack-based approach allows it to potentially find a solution much faster if it happens to be down a "deep" path, but it can also get "lost" in very deep or infinite paths if not properly managed.

This isn't just an abstract grid! We've transformed a real-world map of Kallio, Helsinki, into a graph. Each **intersection** is a **node**, and each **street segment** connecting them is an **edge**. The **red and green lines** you see represent the "visited edges" – the paths DFS is actively exploring. The **vibrant yellow line**? That's the computed path, highlighting the sequence of streets DFS traversed to reach its destination. It's crucial to note that the complexity and efficiency of DFS on real-world road networks can differ significantly from explicit grid-based graphs. Real roads have varying lengths, one-way restrictions, and complex interconnections, meaning the "depth" and "branching factor" can be highly dynamic, often making DFS a pragmatic choice for pathfinding in such environments.
