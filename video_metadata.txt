**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute fastest* route, not just a good one? This visualization showcases **Bidirectional Dijkstra**, a powerful algorithm that tackles this challenge with elegant efficiency. Instead of a single search radiating outwards, we launch *two* searches simultaneously: one from your starting point and another from your destination. These searches expand outwards, prioritizing nodes (intersections) that offer the lowest accumulated cost (travel time/distance) to reach them. Think of it as two explorers digging tunnels towards each other from opposite ends of a mountain. This approach dramatically reduces the search space compared to a traditional unidirectional Dijkstra, especially in complex graphs like real-world road networks. We achieve this prioritization using a **priority queue**, which ensures we always explore the most promising nodes first, guiding our searches efficiently towards a common meeting point.

Our "Real Map Implementation" brings this theoretical marvel to life in the charming streets of Coyoacán, Mexico City. Here, we've transformed the intricate urban landscape into a graph: each **intersection is a node**, and each **street segment connecting two intersections is an edge**. The edges are weighted, representing factors like travel time, traffic conditions, or street type. In this visualization, the **red and green lines illustrate the "visited edges"** as our two Dijkstra searches progressively explore the graph, discovering potential paths. When the two search frontiers meet, the algorithm constructs the optimal path. The **bright yellow line represents the computed shortest path**, the culmination of our intelligent search. Unlike simple grid-based graphs often used for algorithm demos, real-world road networks are far more complex. They exhibit irregular densities, varying street speeds, and dynamic traffic, making algorithms like Bidirectional Dijkstra exceptionally valuable for achieving true optimization.
