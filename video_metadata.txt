**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute* fastest route? It's not just guessing! Our visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer for shortest path problems. Unlike a standard Dijkstra, which searches outwards from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra simultaneously runs two searches: one forward from your starting point and one backward from your destination. Both searches expand by prioritizing nodes with the lowest accumulated cost (distance) to reach them. This is achieved using **priority queues**, where each node is stored with its current shortest distance found so far. When the two search frontiers meet, we've found our guaranteed shortest path. This intelligent two-pronged approach significantly prunes the search space compared to a single-directional search, especially in large graphs.

This isn't just theoretical! We've brought Graph Theory to life by mapping **Reykjavík (Miðborg)** into a digital network. Each intersection is a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges** with associated weights (travel time or distance). In our visualization, the expanding **red and green lines** represent the edges explored by the forward and backward searches, respectively. The moment these frontiers touch, the algorithm converges, and the **yellow line** illuminates the computed shortest path. You'll notice how this differs from a simple grid. Real-world road networks are inherently complex – irregular intersections, one-way streets, and varying edge weights create a dynamic graph that Bidirectional Dijkstra elegantly navigates, far more efficiently than its single-directional counterpart.
