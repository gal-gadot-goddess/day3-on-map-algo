## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional BFS 📍🏙️

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the fastest route so quickly? It's often a clever application of graph theory! Today, we're visualizing **Bidirectional Breadth-First Search (BFS)** on a real-world map of Seoul's vibrant Seongsu-dong district.

In Bidirectional BFS, we don't just search from point A to point B. Instead, we initiate **two simultaneous BFS searches**: one starting from our origin node (A) and another from our destination node (B). Both searches expand outwards layer by layer, exploring neighbors. Crucially, each search prioritizes nodes that are closer to its respective starting point. This is typically managed using **queues** for each search direction (let's call them `queue_A` and `queue_B`). As nodes are visited, they are marked, and their neighbors are added to their respective queues if not already visited. The searches continue until a node is visited by *both* searches. The point where they "meet" signifies the discovery of a path, and this intersection dramatically reduces the search space compared to a single-directional BFS, effectively cutting the search time in half in ideal scenarios.

Transforming a bustling city like Seoul's Seongsu-dong into a navigable graph is a fascinating process. Here, **intersections act as our nodes**, and the **streets connecting them are our edges**. For this visualization, the **red and green lines** represent the "visited edges" as our two BFS searches expand outwards, exploring the graph from both the start and end points. Notice how they intelligently fan out, prioritizing direct connections. The **shimmering yellow line** you see is the **computed path** – the shortest route found where the two search frontiers met. Unlike a simple grid where neighbors are fixed (up, down, left, right), real-world road networks are far more complex. Road layouts, one-way streets, and traffic patterns introduce complexities that make graph algorithms like Bidirectional BFS exceptionally powerful for efficiently navigating these intricate landscapes.
