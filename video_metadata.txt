**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional BFS** 🗺️✨

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the shortest routes so quickly? Today, we're diving deep into a powerful algorithm: Bidirectional Breadth-First Search (BFS). Unlike a traditional BFS that starts from a single source and expands outwards, Bidirectional BFS cleverly initiates *two* searches simultaneously – one from the starting point and another from the destination. It intelligently prioritizes exploring neighbors based on their distance from their respective origins, typically using a **queue** for each search. As these two search frontiers expand, they aim to "meet in the middle." This convergence significantly reduces the search space, drastically cutting down the computational time compared to a single-directional BFS, especially in large graphs.

Witnessing this algorithm come to life in the charming streets of Lisbon's Belém district is truly fascinating! We've transformed the city's road network into a graph, where intersections are our **nodes** and the streets connecting them are our **edges**. In this visualization, the red and green lines represent the expanding search frontiers of our two BFS instances, diligently exploring the graph from opposite ends. When these frontiers meet, the magic happens: the yellow line elegantly materializes, showcasing the optimal path found by connecting the two successful searches. The complexity of this real-world road network, with its irregular intersections and varying road lengths, presents a more nuanced challenge than a simple grid, making the efficiency gains of Bidirectional BFS even more pronounced.
