**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the fastest route? Meet Bidirectional A* – the speed king of pathfinding algorithms! 🚀 Unlike a standard A* that explores outwards from a single start point, Bidirectional A* employs two intelligent searches. One starts from the origin, and the other from the destination. These searches expand concurrently, each guided by a heuristic function that estimates the cost to reach the *opposite* search's starting point. When the two search frontiers meet, we’ve found our optimal path!

Technically, both searches typically utilize a **priority queue** to manage their "open lists" (nodes to be explored). Nodes are prioritized based on their *f-score*, which is the sum of the *g-score* (actual cost from the search's starting point to the current node) and the *h-score* (the heuristic estimate). This ensures that the algorithm always explores the most promising path first. When a node is visited, it's moved to a "closed list" (a set or hash table for efficient lookup). The brilliance lies in the dual search potentially reaching the intersection point much faster than a single-direction search.

Let's bring this to life in the charming streets of Tallinn's Kalamaja district! 🏰 We've transformed this real-world city map into a **graph data structure**. Each **intersection** represents a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**. For visualization, the red and green lines you see are "visited edges" – the path each search has explored. The distinct yellow line is the final, computed shortest path. Notice how on a real road network (a graph with varying edge weights and complex connectivity) the exploration can look quite different from a simple grid. Bidirectional A* shines here, drastically reducing the number of nodes explored compared to a unidirectional search, especially in sprawling environments. The "meeting" of the two search fronts significantly prunes the search space, leading to remarkable efficiency.
