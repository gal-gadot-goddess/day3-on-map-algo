**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how GPS navigates you through winding city streets in near real-time? This visualization showcases **Bidirectional Dijkstra**, a powerhouse algorithm for finding the shortest path on weighted graphs. Unlike a traditional single-direction Dijkstra's, this approach intelligently initiates two searches: one forward from the *source* and one backward from the *destination*. Both searches expand outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on their accumulated distance from their respective starting points. To achieve this optimal expansion, we primarily utilize **priority queues** (often min-heaps) for both forward and backward searches. These data structures ensure that we always explore the node closest to its origin, efficiently managing the "frontier" of exploration.

This demonstration brings the abstract world of Graph Theory to life by transforming the historic Alfama district of Lisbon into a navigable graph. Here, **nodes represent intersections** and **edges are the actual streets**, each assigned a weight (e.g., travel time or distance). The dynamic **red and green lines** you see are the "visited edges," illustrating the expanding exploration from both our starting point (e.g., your current location) and our destination (e.g., a specific landmark in Alfama). The algorithm intelligently stops when the two search frontiers "meet in the middle," significantly reducing the total number of nodes explored compared to a single-direction search, especially in large, complex networks like urban road systems. This makes it the ultimate optimizer for guaranteed shortest path calculations. The complexities can differ greatly from explicit grid implementations due to irregular street layouts, varying edge weights, and the presence of one-way streets, making real-world applications fascinating challenges.
