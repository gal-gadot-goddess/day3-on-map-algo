**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Behold the "speed king" of pathfinding: Bidirectional A*! 👑 This isn't your average search. Instead of one agent forging ahead, we have two intelligent searches, initiating from both the start and goal points, racing towards each other. The core magic lies in their prioritized exploration. Each search maintains an "open set" (typically a priority queue) storing nodes to be evaluated, ordered by their estimated total cost ($f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$). $g(n)$ represents the cost from the source to node $n$, and $h(n)$ is the heuristic estimate of the cost from node $n$ to the goal. By expanding the most promising node (lowest $f(n)$) in each direction simultaneously, Bidirectional A* dramatically reduces the search space. When the two searches meet, the shortest path is found by combining the paths from each search to the meeting point.

Turning a bustling city like Valletta (Il-Belt) into a graph for this algorithm is a fascinating exercise in Graph Theory. Each intersection becomes a "node," and the streets connecting them are "edges." The weight of each edge can represent the travel time, distance, or even traffic conditions. In our visualization, the red and green lines dynamically highlight the "visited edges" – the roads explored by our forward and backward searches respectively. The brilliant yellow line is the computed optimal path, meticulously carved out from the combined efforts of both searches. It's crucial to note the difference in complexity between real-world road networks and explicit grids. While grids offer predictable, uniform edge connections, real roads have varied lengths, one-way streets, and complex junctions, making the heuristic function and graph representation even more critical for efficient pathfinding.
