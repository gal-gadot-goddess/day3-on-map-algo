**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how GPS apps find the fastest route through a maze of streets? This visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, a powerful graph traversal algorithm that's a true optimization champion. Instead of a single search radiating outwards from your starting point, Bidirectional Dijkstra cleverly initiates *two* searches simultaneously: one from your origin and another from your destination. These parallel searches expand by prioritizing nodes closest to their respective starting points, often using a **priority queue** to manage which neighbor to explore next based on the cumulative edge weight. This directed expansion allows the searches to "meet in the middle," dramatically reducing the overall search space compared to a unidirectional Dijkstra. When the two search frontiers intersect, we’ve found our guaranteed shortest path.

This isn't just theoretical! We've implemented Bidirectional Dijkstra on a real-world map of Lisbon's historic Alfama district. Here, **nodes represent street intersections** and **edges are the streets connecting them**, each with an associated "weight" (e.g., travel time, distance, or even traffic congestion). The **red and green lines you see visualize the edges visited** by our forward and backward searches respectively, as they progressively explore the graph. The **brilliant yellow line is the final, computed shortest path** between our chosen start and end points. Unlike a simple grid, real-world road networks are complex and often asymmetric. Bidirectional Dijkstra excels here because it exploits the natural structure of these networks, finding its intersection point much faster than a single-direction search, especially in large and dense urban environments.
