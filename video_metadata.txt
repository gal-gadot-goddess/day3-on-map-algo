## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra 🗺️

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest route, especially in complex urban environments? We're diving deep into a visualization of the **Bidirectional Dijkstra algorithm**, the ultimate optimizer for guaranteed shortest paths in weighted graphs! Unlike a standard Dijkstra that expands outward from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra cleverly initiates *two* simultaneous searches: one forward from the starting point and one backward from the destination. The magic happens when these two search frontiers meet.

At its core, Bidirectional Dijkstra prioritizes exploring nodes with the lowest accumulated cost (distance) from their respective starting points. This is typically achieved using **priority queues**. Each priority queue (one for the forward search, one for the backward) stores tuples of `(cost, node)`, ordered by `cost`. When a node is extracted, its neighbors are considered. For unvisited neighbors, their cost to reach them from the respective start is calculated and they are added to the priority queue. Crucially, Bidirectional Dijkstra needs to check if a newly visited node in one search has already been visited by the *other* search. When this intersection occurs, and the sum of the costs from both searches to that intersection point is minimal, the shortest path is found.

This visualization showcases the **Real Map Implementation** of Bidirectional Dijkstra in the beautiful Sa'dabad region of Tehran. We've translated this urban landscape into a graph: each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**. The **weights** of these edges represent the travel time or distance along those streets. As the algorithm runs, the **red and green lines** illustrate the **"visited edges"** as each search expands its radius. The **yellow line** is the precisely computed **shortest path** that emerges when the two search fronts converge. It's important to note that the complexity of navigating real-world road networks is often far more nuanced than explicit grids. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and traffic patterns introduce complexities that Bidirectional Dijkstra's weighted edge exploration effectively tackles, leading to significantly faster convergence and pathfinding compared to a single-direction search in such intricate scenarios.
