**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Ever wondered how GPS apps find the fastest route through a bustling city? We're visualizing Bidirectional A* in action, the undisputed speed king of pathfinding algorithms! At its core, Bidirectional A* is about efficiency. Instead of a single search radiating outwards from your starting point, it launches *two* intelligent searches simultaneously: one from the start, and another from the destination. These searches "meet in the middle," dramatically reducing the explored search space and computational effort.

The magic lies in how it prioritizes neighbors. Both searches utilize a **priority queue** (often implemented using a **min-heap**) to store "open sets" of nodes to visit. Nodes are prioritized based on their *f-score*, which is the sum of the *g-score* (the cost from the start to that node) and the *h-score* (a heuristic estimate of the cost from that node to the destination). By intelligently choosing the most promising nodes to expand, both searches make rapid progress. When the two search frontiers meet, a path is found. This approach is particularly effective for map-based pathfinding where the search space can be vast.

This visualization brings graph theory to life on the charming, winding streets of Lisbon's Alfama district! We've transformed the real-world map into a graph: **nodes represent intersections**, and **edges represent the streets** connecting them. The pulsating red and green lines you see illustrate the "visited edges" as each search expands its territory, exploring the graph. Notice how the searches are working towards each other! The bright yellow line finally reveals the computed optimal path, meticulously crafted by our bidirectional search. Unlike simple grid-based pathfinding, real-world road networks present unique challenges. Road networks are often irregular, with varying edge weights (travel times due to traffic, speed limits, one-way streets), making sophisticated algorithms like Bidirectional A* crucial for finding efficient and accurate routes. This is graph theory, unboxed and running in the real world!
