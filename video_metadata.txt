**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps *try* to find the quickest route? 🤔 Let's dive into the **Greedy Best-First Search algorithm**, visualized on the vibrant streets of Lima's Barranco district! 🇵🇪 At its core, Greedy Best-First is an informed search algorithm. It prioritizes exploring nodes based on a heuristic function that estimates the *cost* from the current node to the target. Think of it as always taking the step that *looks* most promising right now! It uses a **priority queue** to manage its frontier of nodes to visit, always extracting the node with the lowest heuristic cost. While this makes it incredibly fast, it's important to note: this "greedy" approach doesn't guarantee the globally optimal path. It might take you down a seemingly direct but ultimately longer route if a better, less direct path existed!

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the complex urban landscape of Barranco into a tangible graph. 🏙️ Each **intersection** represents a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**. In this visualization, the red and green lines show the edges the algorithm has "visited" or explored in its quest. The bright yellow line? That's the computed path from our starting point to the target destination, a direct result of the Greedy Best-First strategy. Unlike explicit grid-based pathfinding where all adjacent moves have a uniform cost, real-world road networks introduce significant complexity. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and traffic congestion mean edge weights aren't static. Our algorithm's heuristic here would typically be the straight-line (Euclidean) distance to the target, giving it that "straight-line" push. This highlights the trade-off: speed vs. optimality when dealing with the dynamic nature of real-world navigation.
