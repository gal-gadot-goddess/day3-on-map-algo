### Decoding Greedy Best-First

Greedy Best-First is an informed search algorithm that aims to reach the target node as quickly as possible. Its core strategy is to always expand the node that appears closest to the goal, using a heuristic function. It doesn't consider the cost of the path taken *so far* (unlike Dijkstra's or A*). This "greedy" approach means it prioritizes exploration based solely on the estimated distance to the goal. Technically, it utilizes a **priority queue** to store nodes, ordered by their heuristic evaluation. When selecting the next node to expand, it simply picks the one with the minimum heuristic value from the priority queue. This makes it incredibly fast when the heuristic is well-chosen, but it can sometimes lead it down suboptimal paths, or even into dead ends if the heuristic is misleading.

### From Streets to Nodes: Our Real Map Graph

Translating a real-world city like Bucharest (Sector 6 - Militari) into a graph involves abstracting its structure. **Nodes** represent key locations, most commonly **intersections**, and **edges** represent the **streets** connecting them. In our visualization, the **red and green lines** illustrate the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph. These represent the streets the algorithm has considered and traversed. The **yellow line** highlights the **computed path** from our starting point to the target. It's important to note that implementing pathfinding on real roads presents unique complexities compared to explicit grids. Road networks are often irregular, with varying speeds, one-way streets, and complex junction layouts, all of which require careful consideration when defining edge weights and the heuristic function.
