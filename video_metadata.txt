**Visualizing Depth-First Search (DFS) on the Streets of Edinburgh! 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿**

**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization showcases Depth-First Search (DFS) in action, using the intricate street grid of Edinburgh's New Town as our real-world graph! 🗺️ DFS operates by exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When presented with multiple unvisited neighbors from a given node (intersection), DFS arbitrarily picks one and recursively calls itself on that neighbor. This "plunge deep" strategy is typically implemented using a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly via the call stack in recursion). Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS's strength lies in its speed and ability to find a solution quickly, though it can sometimes get "lost" down a very long, unproductive path before eventually exploring other branches.

In this "Real Map Implementation," we've transformed the familiar layout of Edinburgh's New Town into a graph data structure. Each intersection is a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges**. The red and green lines you see visualize the exploration process: red signifies edges that have been visited and backtracked from, while green shows the current active exploration path. The bright yellow line dynamically traces the **computed path** discovered by our DFS algorithm. It's important to note that applying graph algorithms to real-world road networks introduces complexities beyond simple grids. Factors like one-way streets, traffic conditions, and differing street lengths (edge weights) can significantly impact traversal efficiency and the optimal path found, making the challenge more dynamic than a uniform grid traversal.
