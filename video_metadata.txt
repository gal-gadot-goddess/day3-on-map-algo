**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra 🗺️✨**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute* fastest route? Meet Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer in graph theory! Instead of a single search radiating outwards from your starting point, this algorithm cleverly initiates two simultaneous searches: one from your origin (A) and another from your destination (B). These searches expand their "visited edges" (visualized as red and green lines here) by prioritizing the closest nodes based on edge weights (street lengths and traffic conditions, though simplified in this demo). It uses **priority queues** to efficiently explore the graph, always picking the path that promises the shortest distance at that moment. The magic happens when these two expanding frontiers meet! This intersection significantly reduces the search space compared to a unidirectional Dijkstra, guaranteeing the shortest path with remarkable speed.

In this visualization, we've transformed Barcelona's iconic Eixample district into a real-world graph. Each **intersection is a node**, and each **street segment is an edge** with associated weights. The red and green lines represent the expanding search spaces from our start and end points, respectively. When these two colored explorations converge, the algorithm identifies the meeting node and reconstructs the optimal path, highlighted by the yellow line. Unlike simple grid-based graphs where complexity scales predictably, real-world road networks introduce complexities like one-way streets, varying traffic densities, and complex intersection layouts, making Bidirectional Dijkstra's efficiency a true game-changer.
