## Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First

Witness Greedy Best-First in action! 🚀 This algorithm prioritizes exploration based on a heuristic, aiming to reach the target as directly as possible. At each step, it examines the neighbors of the current node and selects the one that *appears* to be closest to the goal. Critically, Greedy Best-First utilizes a **priority queue** to manage its open list. Unlike Breadth-First Search (which uses a standard queue for level-by-level exploration) or Depth-First Search (which might use a stack), the priority queue ensures that nodes with the most promising heuristic values are explored first, effectively "pulling" the algorithm towards the destination. The heuristic used here is typically the **Euclidean distance** or **Manhattan distance** from the current node to the target.

Our visualization brings this concept to life on the intricate streets of Valencia's Ciutat Vella! 🗺️ We've transformed this historic district into a graph where **nodes represent road intersections** and **edges represent the streets connecting them**. The red and green lines you see illustrate the algorithm's exploration path – the edges it has "visited" as it searches. The bright yellow line signifies the path that Greedy Best-First has computed to reach the target. While this approach is often swift due to its directness, it's crucial to understand that it's a **greedy strategy**. It doesn't guarantee the *shortest* path, as it can get "stuck" in locally optimal but globally suboptimal routes. The complexities of real-world road networks, with varying street lengths, one-way systems, and unexpected dead ends, further highlight how even seemingly simple algorithms can reveal fascinating pathfinding challenges.
