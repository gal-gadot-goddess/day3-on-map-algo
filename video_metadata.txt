**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Witness the power of optimization in action! Our latest visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, a sophisticated algorithm designed to find the *guaranteed shortest path* between two points with unparalleled efficiency. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one from the starting point and another from the destination. These searches expand outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on their accumulated path cost (f-score) using a **priority queue**. This queue ensures that the algorithm always explores the most promising nodes first, effectively exploring the graph from both ends. By meeting in the middle, it significantly reduces the search space compared to a single-directional search, making it the ultimate optimizer for route finding.

In this "Real Map Implementation," we've transformed the intricate urban landscape of Lagos – specifically Yaba and Lagos Island – into a navigable graph. Each **intersection** represents a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**, each assigned a weight (representing travel time or distance). The vibrant red and green lines you see illustrate the **visited edges** of our expanding searches. As these searches converge, the calculated shortest path emerges as a distinct **yellow line**. It's crucial to understand that real-world road networks, with their complex connectivity and varying traffic conditions, present a far more challenging and dynamic problem than a simple grid. This implementation highlights how graph theory principles can be powerfully applied to solve complex, real-world routing challenges.
