**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First 🗺️🏃‍♂️**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex environments? In this visualization, we showcase the **Greedy Best-First search algorithm** tackling the intricate streets of Lyon's Presqu'île!

At its core, Greedy Best-First is an informed search strategy. Unlike algorithms that explore exhaustively, Greedy Best-First prioritizes exploring nodes that appear to be closest to the *goal* based on a heuristic function (in our case, the straight-line distance, or Euclidean distance, to the target). It uses a **priority queue** to manage its frontier of nodes to visit. When a node is expanded, its neighbors are evaluated. Each neighbor is assigned a priority based *solely* on its estimated distance to the goal. The algorithm then picks the neighbor with the *lowest* estimated cost and adds it to the priority queue, effectively "greedily" moving towards the target without considering the path cost already incurred to reach that neighbor. This makes it incredibly fast, but as you'll see, not always optimal!

This visualization brings **Graph Theory** to life by mapping the real-world city of Lyon's Presqu'île. We represent intersections as **nodes** and the connecting streets as **edges**. The **red and green lines** you see depict the **visited edges** – the streets the algorithm has explored in its quest. The **yellow line** highlights the **computed path** found by Greedy Best-First. It's fascinating to observe how the algorithm navigates the dense urban fabric. Unlike a simple grid where edge costs are uniform, real-world road networks have varying lengths, one-way streets, and complex intersections, adding layers of complexity to pathfinding. Greedy Best-First's direct approach can be advantageous in scenarios where speed is paramount and a *good enough* path is acceptable, but it might miss shorter routes that involve slightly longer initial detours.
