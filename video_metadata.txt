**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Witness the speed king of pathfinding in action! Our visualization showcases the **Bidirectional A* algorithm**, a sophisticated search strategy that significantly accelerates route discovery, especially in complex environments like city maps. Unlike a traditional single-direction A*, Bidirectional A* employs two independent search agents. One starts from the **source node** (your starting point) and the other from the **goal node** (your destination). Both agents expand their search frontiers simultaneously, intelligently prioritizing neighbors based on a heuristic (estimated cost to the goal) and the actual cost incurred so far. This dual-pronged approach dramatically prunes the search space. Technically, each search typically utilizes a **priority queue** (often a min-heap) to manage its "open list" – the set of nodes to be evaluated – ensuring that the most promising nodes are explored first. The algorithm terminates when the two search frontiers meet, guaranteeing an optimal path.

Transforming a real-world city like **Lagos (specifically Yaba)** into a navigable graph is a prime example of **Graph Theory in action**. We represent **intersections as nodes** and the **connecting streets as edges**. The density and connectivity of these edges on a real map introduce unique challenges compared to a simple grid. In our visualization, the **red and green lines meticulously illustrate the "visited edges"** – the exploration paths of our two A* agents as they intelligently navigate the urban labyrinth. The **vibrant yellow line** signifies the beautifully computed, optimal path that emerged from the intersection of these two searches. The efficiency gains of Bidirectional A* become particularly pronounced on real road networks where the number of potential paths can be astronomically high, and the underlying graph structure is often irregular and less predictable than a uniform grid.
