**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps decide the *fastest* way to get you somewhere? Let's dive into the Greedy Best-First search algorithm, visualized on a real-world map of Reykjavík's Vesturbær district! 🗺️ This algorithm is designed for speed. It prioritizes exploring nodes that appear to be "closest" to the target, based on a heuristic function (in this case, Euclidean distance to the destination). Unlike algorithms like Dijkstra's or A*, Greedy Best-First doesn't look ahead to see if a longer path *now* might lead to a much better overall route later. It simply heads straight for the goal, making it a very direct, but potentially suboptimal, pathfinding strategy. Technically, it often utilizes a **priority queue**, where the priority of a node is determined solely by its heuristic value ($h(n)$). When expanding a node, it considers all its neighbors, evaluates their heuristic cost to the goal, and inserts them into the priority queue accordingly. The node with the lowest heuristic value is always chosen next for expansion.

Our "real map implementation" transforms the urban landscape of Reykjavík into a graph. Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see illustrate the algorithm's exploration process, marking the **visited edges** as it navigates the street network. The bright yellow line represents the computed path from the start to the destination. The complexity of pathfinding on real-world road networks differs significantly from explicit grids. Factors like varying street speeds, one-way streets, turn restrictions, and the sheer density of intersections create a much more dynamic and complex graph structure. While the theoretical complexity of Greedy Best-First can be analyzed on simpler graphs, its performance and optimality in a chaotic, real-world environment like a city can be quite different. It's a fantastic example of applying graph theory concepts to tangible, everyday problems!
