**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization showcases Depth-First Search (DFS) in action, using the winding streets of Marrakesh's Medina as our backdrop! 🗺️

DFS operates by exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. Think of it like a curious explorer who, upon reaching a junction, picks one path and follows it to its absolute end. If that path leads to a dead end or a place already visited, it backtracks to the last junction and tries another unvisited path. Technically, DFS typically uses a **stack** (either implicitly through recursion or explicitly) to keep track of the paths it's exploring. When it encounters a node, it pushes its unvisited neighbors onto the stack and then pops one to visit next. This "depth-first" approach prioritizes going deeper into one branch before exploring its siblings. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which explores level by level using a queue, DFS can be more memory-efficient for deep graphs but might take a longer route if the "goal" is closer to the starting point.

Our "real map implementation" transforms the intricate labyrinth of Marrakesh's Medina into a computational graph. Each intersection represents a **node**, and the streets connecting them are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see highlight actively **visited edges**, demonstrating DFS's traversal as it explores potential routes. The vibrant yellow line showcases the **computed path** (though in this strict DFS visualization, it's often showing the successful route found by a modified DFS or a path reconstruction after the search). It's important to note the complexity difference: in explicit grid-based graphs perfect for algorithmic understanding, all connections are predictable. However, real-world road networks, like the Medina, are far more dynamic and often lack simple grid structures, leading to more unpredictable traversal patterns and potentially higher traversal costs if edges represent travel time or distance.
