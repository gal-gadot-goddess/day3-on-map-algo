**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? 🗺️ Dive into our latest visualization showcasing Depth-First Search (DFS) in action, brought to life on the streets of Reykjavík's Miðborg district!

In this implementation, DFS operates by systematically exploring paths as deeply as possible before it *backtracks*. Think of it like a curious explorer who chooses one direction and keeps going until they hit a dead end or a previously visited landmark. It doesn't necessarily find the shortest path, but its methodical, "go deep first" approach allows it to discover connected components efficiently. For its core operations, DFS leverages a **stack** (either implicitly through recursion or explicitly using a data structure). When exploring neighbors, it prioritizes the first unvisited neighbor it encounters, effectively plunging down that branch of the "tree" of possibilities. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which would use a queue to explore level by level, DFS's stack-based nature dictates its deep dive. This can lead it down long, winding paths, which is why it's famously described as potentially "getting lost" but often achieving its goal with impressive speed!

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms Reykjavík's Miðborg into a tangible graph. Each **intersection** is a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see represent the **visited edges** as DFS actively explores the road network – the green showing the current exploration path and red marking previously explored segments. The bright yellow line? That's the computed path found by DFS! It's crucial to note that applying graph algorithms to real-world road networks often presents unique complexities compared to simple grid-based graphs. Real roads have one-way restrictions, varying speeds, and varying traversal costs (time/distance), which can influence the *practical* efficiency and pathfinding outcomes. While the core DFS logic remains the same, these real-world factors would require enhancements like edge weights and directional constraints for a truly optimized navigation system, making this visualization a powerful illustration of the foundational concepts.
