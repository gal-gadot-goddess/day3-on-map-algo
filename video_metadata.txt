**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how pathfinding algorithms navigate complex networks? Let's dive into a real-world application of Greedy Best-First search, visualized on the streets of Istanbul's vibrant Kadıköy district! 🇹🇷 This algorithm operates by always choosing the neighbor that *appears* closest to the target node. It uses a **priority queue** to store and retrieve the most promising nodes to explore next. At each step, it expands the node that has the lowest heuristic cost to the goal. The heuristic function (often the straight-line or Euclidean distance in a geographical context) is key here; it guides the search directly towards the destination without necessarily considering the cost of the path already taken. Think of it as a eager explorer always taking the shortest perceived hop towards their objective!

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the intricate road network of Kadıköy into a graph data structure. Here, **nodes represent intersections**, and **edges represent the streets connecting them**. The visualization showcases the algorithm's journey: the red and green lines highlight the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores potential routes, expanding its search space. The bright yellow line ultimately represents the **computed path** found by Greedy Best-First. Unlike abstract grid-based pathfinding where all edge costs are uniform, real-world road networks introduce complexities like varying street lengths, one-way systems, and traffic conditions, making heuristic accuracy even more critical for efficiency. This direct, greedy approach prioritizes speed, often finding a good-enough solution quickly, but it's important to remember it doesn't guarantee the absolute shortest path due to its myopic decision-making.
