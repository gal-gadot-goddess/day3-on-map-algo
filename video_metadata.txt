**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how algorithms find paths, and what trade-offs they make? In this visualization, we're showcasing the **Greedy Best-First Search** algorithm applied to the bustling streets of Hanoi's Hoàn Kiếm District. The core logic of Greedy Best-First is its direct approach: it always expands the node that is **closest to the goal state**, based on a heuristic function (in this case, a simple straight-line distance). It doesn't look ahead at the cost of reaching intermediate nodes, making it incredibly fast but potentially suboptimal. Technically, it utilizes a **priority queue** to efficiently manage and retrieve the most promising node to explore next, prioritizing neighbors based on their heuristic evaluation. This differs from Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue and explores level by level, or Depth-First Search (DFS) which uses a stack and explores as deeply as possible.

This isn't just an abstract grid; it's a **Real Map Implementation**! We've transformed the complex road network of Hanoi's Hoàn Kiếm District into a graph structure. Each **intersection** is represented as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. The visualization uses **red and green lines** to represent the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, attempting to find the target. The resulting **yellow line** shows the path computed by Greedy Best-First. It's fascinating to see how the algorithm navigates, often making quick decisions based on proximity. The complexity of a real-world road network, with its irregular intersections, one-way streets, and varying road types, presents a far more dynamic challenge than a simple explicit grid, highlighting the nuances of pathfinding in authentic urban environments.
