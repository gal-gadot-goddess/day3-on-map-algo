**Real Map Implementation of Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how your GPS finds the *exact* shortest route? Meet Dijkstra's Algorithm, the gold standard for navigating complex networks like our cities! At its core, Dijkstra works by iteratively exploring the graph, always prioritizing the unvisited node with the *smallest known distance* from the source. It starts with the source node (distance 0) and uses a **priority queue** to efficiently select the next node to process. For each selected node, it examines its neighbors. If a shorter path to a neighbor is found through the current node, the neighbor's distance is updated and it's re-prioritized in the queue. This process continues until the destination is reached, guaranteeing the shortest path by exhaustively weighing every "road."

In this visualization, we've transformed the charming streets of Lisbon's Belém district into a graph. Each intersection represents a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges**, each with a weight (representing travel time or distance). The animation showcases the algorithm's journey: the **red and green lines** highlight "visited edges" as Dijkstra meticulously explores potential paths, and the **yellow line** precisely pinpoints the computed shortest path from our starting point to the destination. Unlike simpler grid-based graphs often used in introductory examples, real-world road networks present unique challenges. They are often sparse, with varying edge weights and complex interdependencies, making efficient graph traversal algorithms like Dijkstra indispensable for accurate navigation.
