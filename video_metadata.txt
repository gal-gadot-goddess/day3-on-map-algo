**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps sometimes take you on a seemingly direct, but perhaps not *optimal*, route? This visualization showcases the **Greedy Best-First Search algorithm** in action, applied to a real-world map of Auckland's Ponsonby district!

**Greedy Best-First Search (GBFS)** is a pathfinding algorithm that prioritizes exploring nodes based on a heuristic function that estimates the cost to reach the target. Unlike Dijkstra's algorithm, which considers the *actual cost* from the start node, GBFS is "greedy" – it looks only at the *estimated future cost*. In our implementation, this means it will always expand the node that appears closest to the destination. GBFS typically uses a **priority queue** to manage its nodes, ordering them by their heuristic value. This allows it to efficiently select the most promising neighbor to explore at each step, aiming for rapid convergence towards the goal. While this can be incredibly fast, its focus solely on the heuristic can lead it to commit to suboptimal paths if the heuristic is misleading, potentially missing a shorter route that requires an initial detour.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the intricate road network of Ponsonby into a **graph data structure**. Each **intersection** is represented as a **node**, and each **street segment** connecting two intersections is an **edge**. The red and green lines you see in the video illustrate the **visited edges** as GBFS explores the graph, progressively marking pathways it has considered. The bright **yellow line** dynamically traces the **computed path** – the route GBFS has decided upon as it moves closer to its destination. Unlike structured grid-based pathfinding, real-world road networks introduce complexities. Edge weights (street lengths and travel times) can vary significantly, and the graph structure is irregular. This makes direct heuristic estimations more challenging and can highlight the trade-offs between speed and optimality that are inherent to algorithms like Greedy Best-First.
