**Understanding the DFS Logic:** At its core, DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. Think of it as a determined explorer who relentlessly follows one path until it hits a dead end or a previously visited location. Then, it intelligently retraces its steps to the last junction where it had unexplored options and dives down another path. DFS typically uses a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the nodes it needs to visit. When it encounters a new, unvisited neighbor, it pushes that neighbor onto the stack and continues its exploration. This "go deep first" strategy makes it incredibly efficient for finding a path or exploring connected components, although it might not always find the *shortest* path initially if multiple paths exist. This is in contrast to algorithms like Breadth-First Search (BFS), which would typically use a **queue** to explore layer by layer.

Finally, the **vibrant yellow line** represents the **computed path** found by our DFS algorithm. It's crucial to note that traversing real-world road networks is significantly more complex than traversing explicit grids.
