**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A* 🗺️📍**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the fastest route? It's a beautiful dance of algorithms! This visualization showcases the **Bidirectional A*** search in action, a true "speed king" in pathfinding. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional A* initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the start node and another backward from the goal node. These searches race towards each other, dramatically reducing the search space. The core of A*'s efficiency lies in its heuristic function ($h(n)$), which estimates the cost from a node to the goal, and its cost-so-far function ($g(n)$), the actual cost from the start to the node. Nodes are prioritized using a **priority queue**, ensuring that the algorithm always expands the node with the lowest $f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$ value, effectively exploring the most promising paths first. The intersection of the two search frontiers signifies the discovery of a path.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the bustling streets of Tbilisi's Vake district into a navigable **graph**. Each intersection acts as a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges**. The **red and green lines** you see represent the "visited edges" – the exploration undertaken by our forward and backward searches, respectively, as they expand their frontiers. The bright **yellow line** is the meticulously computed optimal path, elegantly connecting the start and end points. While A* is incredibly efficient on uniform grids, real-world road networks present unique challenges. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits (edge weights), and complex junction layouts require a robust graph representation and sophisticated heuristic functions to maintain optimal performance. This visualization highlights the power of graph theory applied to tangible, everyday problems!
