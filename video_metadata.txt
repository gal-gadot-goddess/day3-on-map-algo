**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex environments? Here, we're visualizing the **Greedy Best-First search algorithm** in action, using the charming, labyrinthine streets of Lisbon's Alfama district as our playground! 🗺️ The core of Greedy Best-First is its heuristic-driven approach. It prioritizes exploring neighbors that are *closest* to the goal, aiming for speed over optimality. Unlike algorithms that explore exhaustively or consider the path cost so far (like Dijkstra's), Greedy Best-First makes a greedy choice at each step, picking the neighbor that minimizes a heuristic function (often the straight-line distance or Manhattan distance to the target). To achieve this, it typically employs a **priority queue** to efficiently manage and retrieve the "best" node to explore next, based on the heuristic evaluation.

This visualization demonstrates a **Real Map Implementation**, transforming the intricate urban fabric of Alfama into a graph. Each intersection acts as a **node**, and the connecting streets are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see represent the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, progressively narrowing down possibilities. The shimmering yellow line signifies the **computed path** from the start point to our destination. It's crucial to note the complexity difference here: on explicit grids, calculating distances and heuristics is straightforward. However, in real-world road networks, factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and complex intersections introduce significant challenges and make the "best" heuristic more nuanced than simple Euclidean distance. Greedy Best-First excels at finding *a* path quickly, but on such complex, non-uniform graphs, it might not always find the absolute shortest or fastest route – a trade-off for its impressive speed!
