**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Witness the speed king of pathfinding algorithms in action! Our visualization showcases Bidirectional A*, a sophisticated search technique that drastically accelerates route discovery. Instead of one search radiating outwards from the start, Bidirectional A* simultaneously initiates two independent A* searches: one from the starting point and another from the destination. These searches expand outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on a heuristic function (often the Euclidean or Manhattan distance) that estimates the cost to reach the goal, combined with the actual cost incurred so far. The core data structure employed by A* (and thus by both forward and backward searches) is a **priority queue**. This queue intelligently stores and retrieves nodes, always selecting the one with the lowest f-score (g-score + h-score), ensuring that the algorithm explores the most promising paths first. When the two searches meet (i.e., a node is visited by both), a complete path is found. This dual-pronged approach significantly reduces the search space compared to a single A* search, especially in large graphs.

Our implementation brings this algorithmic power to the bustling streets of Kathmandu's Thamel district in Nepal. Transforming a real-world city into a graph involves representing its intricate network of roads. Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** form the **edges** of our graph. The red and green lines you see illustrate the "visited edges" – these are the paths explored by our forward and backward searches, respectively. As these searches converge, the yellow line elegantly depicts the computed shortest path, guiding you through the labyrinthine alleys and thoroughfares. It's crucial to understand that real-world road networks present unique complexities compared to explicit grid-based maps. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and irregular road shapes mean the cost (edge weight) calculation for traversing these edges is more nuanced and can lead to a more complex search landscape, making the efficiency gains of Bidirectional A* even more pronounced.
