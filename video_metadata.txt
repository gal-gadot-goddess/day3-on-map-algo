**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Dive deep into the world of graph traversal with this visualization of Depth-First Search (DFS) in action! 🏙️ Our algorithm prioritizes exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When faced with multiple unvisited neighbors from a given node (an intersection in our city map), DFS employs a stack-like behavior (implicitly or explicitly using a stack data structure) to choose one neighbor and immediately pursue it. It doesn't concern itself with finding the *shortest* path initially; its goal is pure exploration. This makes it exceptionally fast for certain tasks, but it can indeed get "lost" in the maze of possibilities if not managed carefully.

This isn't just a theoretical concept; we've brought it to life on a real map of Santiago, specifically the Providencia commune! 🇨🇱 We've transformed the urban landscape into a graph where each intersection represents a node, and the streets connecting them are the edges. The red and green lines you see are the "visited edges" – the path DFS is currently exploring, marking the streets it has traversed. The vibrant yellow line elegantly highlights the computed path, showcasing the final route determined by the algorithm. It's important to note that real-world road networks introduce complexities beyond simple grid structures. Factors like one-way streets, varying road lengths, and the sheer scale of a city can significantly impact the traversal and the practical efficiency compared to traversing a predefined grid.
