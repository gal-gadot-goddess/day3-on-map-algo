**Real Map Implementation of DFS 🗺️✨**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization demonstrates **Depth-First Search (DFS)** applied to the real-world streets of Reykjavík's Vesturbær district! DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. It prioritizes going *deep* down a single path before considering alternatives. When it encounters a node, it picks an unvisited neighbor and recursively explores from that neighbor. If it reaches a dead end or a node with no unvisited neighbors, it backtracks to the last node that had an unexplored path. DFS typically utilizes a **stack** (either implicitly through recursion or explicitly) to keep track of the nodes to visit and the path taken. This makes it incredibly efficient for finding *a* path, though not necessarily the shortest one.

This isn't just an abstract grid; we've transformed the actual street layout of Vesturbær, Reykjavík into a graph! Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. The animation showcases the DFS algorithm in action: the **red and green lines** represent the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, probing deeper and deeper into the street network. The **vibrant yellow line** highlights the **computed path** found by the DFS. Unlike simple grid-based implementations where all paths are relatively uniform, real-world road networks introduce complexities: one-way streets, varying road lengths, and differing intersection layouts can dramatically influence the algorithm's exploration and the perceived "speed" of traversal. While DFS is known for its speed in exploring possibilities, on a real map, its efficiency is still dependent on the graph's connectivity and the presence of long, winding paths that might require significant backtracking.
