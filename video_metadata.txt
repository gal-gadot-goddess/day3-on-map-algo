## Real Map Implementation of DFS 🗺️

Depth-First Search (DFS) is a powerful graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. In this visualization, we see DFS in action, navigating the streets of Seoul's Jongno-gu. The core logic of DFS prioritizes diving deep into a chosen path. When it encounters a node, it recursively or iteratively explores one of its unvisited neighbors. This is typically implemented using a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly via recursion) to keep track of the nodes to visit. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue and explores level by level, DFS's stack-based approach ensures it delves into the deepest possible path first, making it highly efficient for finding a specific destination or exploring connected components, but it can also get "lost" by following a long, unproductive path before realizing a closer option existed.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the intricate urban landscape of Seoul's Jongno-gu into a graph. Here, **nodes** represent key **intersections**, and **edges** represent the **streets** connecting them. The **red and green lines** meticulously trace the **"visited edges"** as our DFS algorithm explores the network. The **vibrant yellow line** showcases the **computed path** found by the algorithm to reach its destination. It's crucial to note that applying graph algorithms to real-world road networks introduces complexities absent in simpler grid structures. Factors like one-way streets, variable road lengths, traffic congestion, and the sheer scale of interconnections create a more dynamic and challenging graph than a predefined grid, impacting the practical performance and efficiency considerations of DFS compared to its theoretical execution on an explicit grid.
