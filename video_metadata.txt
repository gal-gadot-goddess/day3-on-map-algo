## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A* 🚀

Ever wondered how your navigation app finds the fastest route through a bustling city? Here's a peek under the hood at the **Bidirectional A* algorithm**, also known as the "speed king" of pathfinding! This sophisticated algorithm employs two simultaneous searches, initiating from both the start and goal nodes, and working their way towards each other. At each step, it prioritizes exploring neighbors based on a heuristic function `h(n)` (an estimated cost to reach the goal) and the actual cost incurred so far `g(n)`. The f-score, `f(n) = g(n) + h(n)`, guides the search. To manage these explorations efficiently, Bidirectional A* utilizes two priority queues (often implemented with min-heaps) – one for the forward search (`open_forward`) and one for the backward search (`open_backward`). As these searches expand, they store visited nodes and their associated costs in separate hash tables (`closed_forward` and `closed_backward`) to prevent redundant exploration and to detect when the two search frontiers meet.

In this visualization, we're taking the abstract concept of graph theory and applying it to the vibrant streets of **Mexico City's Coyoacán neighborhood**. We've transformed this real-world urban landscape into a graph where **nodes represent intersections** and **edges represent the streets connecting them**. The **red and green lines** you see dynamically appearing are the "visited edges" – a visual representation of the search frontiers actively exploring the graph from both ends. When these two exploration fronts meet, the algorithm efficiently stitches together the optimal path. The **shimmering yellow line** signifies the precisely computed shortest path, a testament to the algorithm's power. Unlike explicit grid-based pathfinding, real-world road networks present unique challenges. Edge weights (street travel times) can be highly variable due to traffic, one-way streets, and varying road conditions, making accurate heuristic estimations and efficient graph traversal paramount for achieving optimal results.
