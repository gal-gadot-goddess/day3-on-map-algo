**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how algorithms like Depth-First Search (DFS) navigate complex systems? In this visualization, we bring the abstract concept of DFS to life on the streets of Prague, specifically the vibrant Žižkov district! 🇨🇿 At its core, DFS operates by exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. Think of it like a determined explorer who dives down a specific path, meticulously examining every alley and turn. When it hits a dead end or has explored all reachable nodes from that path, it gracefully steps back and tries another unvisited route from a previous junction. This "plunging deep" behavior means it prioritizes a single unvisited neighbor above all others. Technically, DFS is often implemented using a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly through recursion), where the last visited node is the first one to be explored further from. This contrasts with Breadth-First Search (BFS), which typically uses a **queue** to explore layer by layer.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the intricate urban landscape of Žižkov into a digital graph. Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see animating across the map represent **visited edges**, illustrating DFS's exploration process. The red lines show segments that have been traversed and are now being considered for backtracking, while green highlights freshly explored paths. The prominent yellow line meticulously traces the computed path found by DFS. It's crucial to understand that while DFS is generally fast, its performance on real-world road networks can differ significantly from explicit grids. Real roads have inherent complexities: one-way streets, varying traffic conditions (which we're abstracting here, but could be modeled!), and the sheer density of connections create a more dynamic and less predictable graph. This makes for a fascinating practical application of algorithmic thinking!
