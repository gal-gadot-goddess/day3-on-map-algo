## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A\*

Ever wondered how GPS finds the fastest route through a complex city? This visualization demonstrates the **Bidirectional A\* algorithm**, a powerful technique for efficient pathfinding. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional A\* initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the start node and another backward from the goal node. Both searches utilize a **priority queue** to store and explore nodes based on their estimated total cost ($f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$), where $g(n)$ is the actual cost from the start (or goal) to node $n$, and $h(n)$ is the heuristic estimate of the cost from node $n$ to the goal (or start). This dual-pronged approach dramatically reduces the search space. By exploring from both ends, the two search frontiers are much more likely to meet in the middle, significantly outperforming a single unidirectional search, especially in large and complex graphs. The algorithm terminates when the two search trees intersect.

This isn't just theoretical! We've implemented Bidirectional A\* on a real-world map of Marrakesh's Medina. To achieve this, we model the intricate network of streets as a **graph**. Each **intersection** in the Medina becomes a **node**, and each **street segment connecting two intersections** represents an **edge**. The "visited edges" visualized in red and green showcase the exploration undertaken by each of the two simultaneous A\* searches. The final **yellow line** represents the optimal path computed by the algorithm, effectively bridging the gap between the forward and backward searches. Unlike rigid grid-based pathfinding, real-world road networks present unique challenges. Edge weights (street lengths and travel times) are often non-uniform and can even be dynamic (e.g., traffic congestion). Bidirectional A\*'s ability to efficiently navigate these complex, irregular graphs makes it an exceptionally versatile and performant solution for real-world navigation systems.
