**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Witness the power of optimization in action! Our visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, a sophisticated algorithm that drastically improves shortest path calculations. Unlike standard Dijkstra, which expands from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the starting point and one backward from the destination. Each search prioritizes expanding towards the other, effectively meeting in the middle. This is achieved by using priority queues for both the forward and backward searches. These priority queues store nodes along with their cumulative path weights, ensuring that the algorithm always explores the most promising paths first. When the searches "meet" (i.e., a node has been visited by both the forward and backward searches), we've found the guaranteed shortest path. The beauty lies in how it intelligently prunes the search space, reducing the overall computational effort.

This implementation vividly illustrates the concept of graph theory applied to real-world infrastructure. We've modeled a section of Reykjavík, specifically the Hlíðar/Seltjarnarnes edge, where intersections act as nodes and streets represent the weighted edges. The red and green lines you see dynamically expanding represent the "visited edges" by our forward and backward searches, respectively, as they explore the urban graph. The vibrant yellow line culminating at the end signifies the computed shortest path between our chosen start and end points. While explicit grid-based pathfinding can be predictable, real-world road networks present a far more complex and irregular graph. This complexity is precisely where Bidirectional Dijkstra truly shines, offering a significantly more efficient solution compared to its unidirectional counterpart, especially in large and intricate maps.
