**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra** 🗺️✨

Ever wondered how the fastest guaranteed shortest path is found? Meet Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer for graph traversal. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, this algorithm elegantly launches two simultaneous searches: one from the *source* and another from the *destination*. Each search maintains its own priority queue (typically a min-heap for weighted graphs) to explore neighboring nodes based on their accumulated cost. When the frontiers of these two searches meet, we've effectively "closed the loop" and identified the optimal path. This strategy significantly reduces the search space by almost halving the explored area compared to a single Dijkstra, making it incredibly efficient for large, complex graphs.

In this visualization, we’ve transformed the intricate street network of Havana's Vedado district into a real-world graph. Each intersection represents a **node**, and each street segment connecting them is an **edge** with a weight corresponding to its travel time or distance. Observe the visualization: the shimmering **red and green lines** illustrate the bidirectional exploration – the red representing the forward search from our starting point, and the green signifying the backward search from our destination. The **yellow line** vividly showcases the computed shortest path that emerges precisely where these two intelligent searches converge. It's crucial to note that real-world road networks, with their irregular layouts and varying edge densities, present a more complex challenge than a uniform grid. Bidirectional Dijkstra's ability to adapt and efficiently navigate these complexities makes it a powerful tool for practical applications like GPS navigation.
