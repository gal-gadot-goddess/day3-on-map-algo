**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest route? Let's explore the **Greedy Best-First Search** algorithm, visualized on a real-world map of Kathmandu's vibrant Thamel district! 🗺️ Our visualization highlights how Greedy Best-First operates: it's characterized by its direct, goal-oriented approach. At each step, it selects the *neighboring node* that appears closest to the target, based on a heuristic evaluation function (often Manhattan or Euclidean distance in grid-based scenarios, but adapted for real-world road networks). Crucially, this algorithm doesn't look ahead to see if a seemingly optimal first step might lead to a dead-end or a much longer overall path. It prioritizes immediate "greed" towards the goal. To manage its exploration, Greedy Best-First typically utilizes a **Priority Queue**. This data structure is essential because it allows us to efficiently retrieve the node with the lowest heuristic cost (i.e., the "closest" neighbor to the target) at each iteration, ensuring we're always chasing the most promising lead.

This isn't just an abstract grid! We've transformed the bustling streets of Thamel into a **graph**, where **nodes** represent key intersections and **edges** are the interconnected streets. The vibrant **red and green lines** you see trace the algorithm's exploration – the edges it has "visited" in its search for the optimal path. The pulsating **yellow line** represents the final path computed by Greedy Best-First. It's important to note that applying graph search algorithms to real-world road networks introduces complexities not present in explicit grids. Street layouts can be irregular, with one-way streets, complex intersections, and varying road conditions, all of which add layers of constraint and computational challenge beyond simple coordinate-based heuristics. While Greedy Best-First is exceptionally fast due to its single-minded focus, its real-world effectiveness can be limited by its tendency to miss the truly optimal route if a local "best" choice deviates significantly from the global optimum.
