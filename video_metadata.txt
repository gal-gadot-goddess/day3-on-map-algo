**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A\*** 🗺️✨

Witness the "speed king" of pathfinding in action! Our visualization showcases the Bidirectional A* algorithm, a powerful optimization of the classic A* search. Instead of a single search radiating outwards, Bidirectional A* simultaneously launches two intelligent searches from both the start and goal nodes. Each search maintains its own open set (often a **priority queue** to efficiently retrieve nodes with the lowest f-cost) and closed set (to track visited nodes and prevent redundant exploration). The heuristic function (e.g., Euclidean or Manhattan distance) is crucial for both searches, guiding them towards each other. When the two search frontiers meet, the optimal path is found by merging the two partial paths at the intersection point, leveraging the stored parent pointers from each search's respective data structures. This parallel approach dramatically reduces the search space explored, making it exceptionally efficient for large graphs.

This isn't just a theoretical exercise; it's a **Real Map Implementation** in the heart of Dublin's Rathmines! We've transformed the intricate network of streets and intersections into a graph data structure. Each **intersection** is a **node**, and each **street segment connecting two intersections** is an **edge**. In our visualization, the pulsing red and green lines represent the expanding "visited edges" of our two concurrent searches, actively exploring the graph's topology. The vibrant yellow line then reveals the computed shortest path, meticulously discovered by these two intelligent agents converging. Unlike uniform grids, real-world road networks exhibit varying edge costs (travel times, traffic), introducing complexities that Bidirectional A*'s adaptive search gracefully handles, making it ideal for practical navigation.
