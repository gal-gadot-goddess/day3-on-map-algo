**Real Map Implementation of DFS 🗺️🚶‍♂️**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? This visualization brings Depth-First Search (DFS) to life on the streets of Porto's Campanhã district! 🇵🇹

DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. Think of it like exploring a maze: you pick a direction and keep going until you hit a dead end, then you backtrack to the last junction where you had an unexplored path and try a different route. This "depth-first" approach means it prioritizes going deeper into a path. At its core, DFS often utilizes a **stack** (either explicitly managed or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the nodes to visit and the path taken. When it encounters a new, unvisited neighbor, it pushes that neighbor onto the stack and continues the exploration from there. If all neighbors of a node have been visited, the algorithm pops the current node from the stack and backtracks to the previous node to explore its remaining unvisited neighbors. This makes it exceptionally fast for finding *a* solution, though not necessarily the shortest one, and can sometimes lead it down very long, winding paths before it discovers the target.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the urban landscape of Porto (Campanhã) into a tangible graph! 🏙️ Each intersection is a **node**, and the streets connecting them are the **edges**. The vibrant red and green lines you see represent the **visited edges** as DFS actively explores the graph, marking the pathways it has traversed. The bright yellow line showcases the **computed path** – the successful route found by the algorithm. Unlike a simple grid where each cell has a fixed number of neighbors (typically 4 or 8), real-world road networks are far more dynamic. The **degree** (number of outgoing edges) of a node can vary drastically, with busy intersections having many more connections than quieter residential streets. This complexity, along with factors like one-way streets and one-way traversal of edges (you can only travel down a street in one direction), adds unique challenges and necessitates a robust graph representation to accurately model the city's connectivity. This makes DFS a powerful tool for understanding how algorithms can navigate and find routes within such intricate, real-world systems.
