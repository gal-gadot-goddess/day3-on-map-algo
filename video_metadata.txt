**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find *a* path, even if it's not always the *absolute shortest*? Enter Greedy Best-First Search! This algorithm operates on a simple, yet powerful, heuristic: it greedily chooses the neighbor node that *appears* to be closest to the target, based on a defined evaluation function. In our visualization, this means the algorithm prioritizes exploring roads that, at that moment, seem to lead most directly towards our destination in Santa Teresa, Rio de Janeiro. Technically, this approach often utilizes a **priority queue** to efficiently manage and retrieve the node with the most promising heuristic value at each step. Unlike algorithms like Breadth-First Search (BFS) which guarantees the shortest path on unweighted graphs, Greedy Best-First prioritizes speed and immediate progress, often at the expense of optimality.

Our visualization brings this abstract concept to life by transforming the intricate streets of Santa Teresa, Rio de Janeiro, into a tangible graph! Each **intersection** represents a **node**, and each **street segment** connecting two intersections is an **edge**. The red and green lines you see dynamically visualize the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, essentially tracing its "thought process" as it navigates through the city's network. The brilliant yellow line showcases the **computed path** that Greedy Best-First ultimately determined. It's crucial to understand that real-world road networks present complexities far beyond simple grid-based graphs. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, traffic conditions, and the sheer non-uniformity of street layouts dramatically influence pathfinding, making heuristic-based approaches like Greedy Best-First a compelling choice for quickly finding *a* viable route when absolute shortest path isn't the sole objective.
