**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps *could* find routes? Let's dive into the **Greedy Best-First Search algorithm** in action! 🗺️ This algorithm is all about speed. At each step, it greedily expands the node that appears closest to the target, using a heuristic function to estimate the distance. Think of it as always taking the most promising step *towards* the goal, without looking too far ahead. For our implementation, we're using a **priority queue** to efficiently manage the open list of nodes to explore, ensuring we always pull out the node with the lowest heuristic cost first. While incredibly fast, remember: this greedy approach prioritizes reaching the goal quickly over finding the absolute shortest path, potentially leading to suboptimal routes in complex scenarios.

In this visualization, we've transformed a section of San Francisco's SoMa district into a real-world graph! 🌉 Each **intersection** is a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see represent the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, trying out different paths. The bright yellow line signifies the **computed path** from our starting point to the target destination. Navigating a real city presents unique complexities compared to a simple grid. Factors like one-way streets, varying traffic speeds (which we abstract here for simplicity, but could be incorporated), and the sheer density of intersections mean that the "straightest line" isn't always the fastest or even a valid route. Our visualization simplifies these complexities to highlight the core logic of Greedy Best-First.
