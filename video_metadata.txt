**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional BFS**

Ever wondered how pathfinding algorithms work beyond just grids? In this visualization, we're bringing **Bidirectional Breadth-First Search (BFS)** to life on a real-world map of Miraflores, Lima! The core idea behind Bidirectional BFS is remarkably elegant: instead of a single search radiating outwards from your starting point, we initiate *two* independent BFS searches simultaneously. One starts from your origin (Point A), and the other begins from your destination (Point B). Both searches expand level by level, exploring their respective neighborhoods. Crucially, Bidirectional BFS intelligently prioritizes neighbors by expanding outwards from the side that has explored fewer nodes, aiming to keep the search frontiers balanced. This is typically achieved using **queues** for each of the two searches to maintain the FIFO (First-In, First-Out) order of exploration, ensuring we find the shortest path by visiting nodes in increasing order of distance from their respective start points.

Translating a city like Lima (Miraflores) into a graph involves treating **intersections as nodes** and the **streets connecting them as edges**. Each edge can be thought of as a potential step in our journey. In this visualization, the **red and green lines represent the edges being "visited"** by our two separate BFS searches as they explore the graph from opposite ends. The **bright yellow line vividly illustrates the computed shortest path** found when the two search frontiers successfully "meet" in the middle. This intersection point is where the path from A to B is completed. The advantage of Bidirectional BFS becomes apparent here; by searching from both directions, the total number of nodes explored is significantly reduced compared to a single BFS, often cutting the search time in half. This efficiency is particularly pronounced in complex, real-world road networks, which are far from the simple, explicit grids you might see in traditional algorithm tutorials, where edge weights might be uniform.
