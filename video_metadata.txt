**Real Map Implementation of DFS** 🏙️🚶‍♀️

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex spaces? This visualization showcases Depth-First Search (DFS) in action, mapping the intricate streets of New York's SoHo district! At its core, DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When faced with multiple unvisited neighbors, DFS arbitrarily picks one to explore next, diving deep into that path. This "go deep first" strategy is typically implemented using a **stack** (explicitly or implicitly via recursion). Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a **queue** to explore layer by layer, DFS's inherent mechanism prioritizes depth, making it efficient for finding *a* path quickly, even if it's not the shortest.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the urban labyrinth of SoHo into a graph where **nodes represent intersections** and **edges represent the streets connecting them**. The dynamic red and green lines visually trace the algorithm's exploration – red marking a visited edge that led to a dead end or already explored territory, and green highlighting the current path of discovery. The bright yellow line elegantly depicts the final computed path, demonstrating DFS's ability to find a route through the city. It's fascinating to observe how the inherent complexity and connectivity of a real-world road network, unlike a perfect grid, can significantly influence traversal performance and the nature of the paths discovered by algorithms like DFS.
