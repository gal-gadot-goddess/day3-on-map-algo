**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps *think*? Let's dive into the Greedy Best-First search algorithm visualized on the actual streets of Cape Town! 🗺️ This algorithm is characterized by its direct approach: it greedily chooses the path that appears to lead *closest* to the target at each step, without looking ahead beyond its immediate neighbors. Think of it like always taking the next street that *seems* to point you in the right direction. Technically, it utilizes a **priority queue** (or a min-heap) to store its potential next moves. Each element in the priority queue is prioritized based on a heuristic function, typically the estimated distance (like Euclidean or Manhattan distance) from the current node to the goal. This ensures that the most promising neighbor is always explored first. Unlike BFS (which uses a queue for breadth-first exploration) or DFS (which uses a stack for depth-first exploration), Greedy Best-First's focus on the heuristic makes it potentially very quick, but also prone to suboptimal routes if the heuristic isn't perfectly aligned with the true shortest path.

In this "Real Map Implementation," we've transformed the vibrant streets of Cape Town's Woodstock into a tangible graph. Each intersection represents a **node**, and the connecting streets are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see flashing across the map illustrate the **visited edges** as the algorithm explores possibilities, trying to find a way to the destination. The final computed path, highlighted in yellow, is the route Greedy Best-First arrived at. It's important to note the complexity here: mapping a real-world city introduces nuances absent in simple grid-based graphs. Factors like one-way streets, varying road lengths, traffic patterns (which aren't considered by this basic algorithm), and irregular road networks significantly increase the complexity of accurately representing and navigating the graph compared to a perfectly uniform grid. This visualization highlights the core logic of Greedy Best-First, demonstrating its swift, direct traversal, even if it doesn't guarantee the absolute shortest path.
