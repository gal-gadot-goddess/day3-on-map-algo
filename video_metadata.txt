## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A* 🚀

Witness the "speed king" of pathfinding in action! This visualization showcases the Bidirectional A* algorithm navigating the charming streets of Reykjavík's Vesturbær district. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional A* employs two intelligent searches: one originating from the start node and the other from the goal node. These searches move towards each other, effectively shrinking the search space by more than half in many cases. At each step, both forward and backward searches prioritize neighbors based on a heuristic function (h(n)) – an estimated cost to reach the goal – combined with the actual cost incurred from the starting point (g(n)). This f(n) = g(n) + h(n) value is managed using priority queues, ensuring that the most promising nodes are explored first. The magic happens when the two search frontiers meet, guaranteeing the shortest path with significantly reduced computational overhead.

This isn't just abstract code; it's a real-world application of Graph Theory. We've transformed Reykjavík into a graph where intersections are nodes and streets are edges. The vibrant red and green lines you see represent the "visited edges" – the exploration of the graph by our two search agents. When these exploration frontiers finally intersect, the algorithm can reconstruct the optimal path, highlighted in yellow. Implementing pathfinding on real road networks presents unique challenges compared to simple grid-based scenarios. Street layouts are often irregular, with varying speeds, one-way restrictions, and complex junction geometries. This requires careful consideration of edge weights and the design of effective heuristic functions to ensure efficiency and accuracy in navigating the intricate urban landscape.
