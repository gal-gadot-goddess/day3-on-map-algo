**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how GPS apps find the *absolute fastest* route? Meet Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimization engine! Unlike standard Dijkstra's single-direction search, this algorithm intelligently launches two simultaneous searches: one from your starting point and another from your destination. Both searches expand outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on their current shortest distance from their respective origins. This is typically achieved using **priority queues**, which efficiently store and retrieve nodes with the smallest accumulated edge weights. The algorithm terminates the moment these two search frontiers meet, drastically reducing the search space and guaranteeing the shortest path with remarkable speed.

Our "Real Map Implementation" brings this powerful algorithm to life in the urban landscape of Oslo's Sagene district! We transform the city into a graph where intersections are **nodes** and streets are **edges**. These edges are weighted by real-world travel times (or distances, depending on optimization goals). In the visualization, the expanding red and green lines represent the visited edges of our forward and backward searches, respectively. They are meticulously chosen by the priority queues at each step. When these searches converge, the brilliant yellow line materializes, highlighting the optimal path. It's crucial to note that navigating real-world road networks, with their inherent complexities and varying edge weights (traffic, speed limits), presents a significantly more nuanced challenge than traversing a simple, uniform grid.
