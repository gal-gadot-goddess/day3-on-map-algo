**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional BFS**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the fastest route? This visualization showcases a real-world application of Bidirectional Breadth-First Search (BFS)! In Bidirectional BFS, we don't just start one search from our origin. Instead, we initiate *two* independent BFS searches simultaneously: one from the starting point and another from the destination. Both searches explore the graph outwards, layer by layer. When these two search frontiers meet, we've found our path! Each search typically prioritizes neighbors based on their distance from their respective starting points, using **Queues** to manage the order of nodes to visit. This simultaneous exploration is what drastically reduces the search space and, consequently, the computation time.

This isn't just abstract theory; it's a **Real Map Implementation**! We've transformed the streets of Odessa's Primorsky District into a graph. Think of each intersection as a **node** and each street connecting them as an **edge**. The red and green lines you see represent the **visited edges** as our bidirectional searches expand. The exciting part is when these red and green frontiers collide – that's where the magic happens! The brightly colored **yellow line** signifies the optimal path meticulously computed by our algorithm. Unlike grid-based BFS where edge traversal is uniform, real-world road networks have complexities like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and traffic congestion, making graph traversal an even more compelling challenge and a crucial application for algorithms like Bidirectional BFS.
