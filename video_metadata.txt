**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how your GPS finds the quickest route? Dive into the elegance of **Bidirectional Dijkstra's Algorithm**! Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, this sophisticated optimization technique launches *two* searches simultaneously: one forward from your starting point and another backward from your destination. Each search expands outwards by exploring neighbors with the lowest accumulated cost, prioritizing nodes that are closest to their respective origins. This is typically achieved using a **priority queue**, which efficiently manages nodes based on their current shortest known distance. When the two search frontiers meet, the algorithm has guaranteed the shortest path.

Our **Real Map Implementation** showcases this powerful algorithm in action within the charming streets of Reykjavík's Miðborg district. Here, graph theory becomes tangible: each intersection represents a **node**, and the connecting streets are **edges** with associated weights (travel time/distance). The vibrant red and green lines you see illustrate the "visited edges" as both searches expand and explore the city's graph. The final yellow line, the star of the show, is the meticulously computed shortest path. This real-world application highlights how graph structures can model complex networks, and importantly, how the efficiency of Bidirectional Dijkstra often outperforms its single-direction counterpart, especially on sparse graphs like road networks, compared to denser, explicit grid structures.
