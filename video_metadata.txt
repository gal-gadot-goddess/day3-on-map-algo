## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra

Ever wondered how your navigation app finds the *absolute* fastest route? Meet Bidirectional Dijkstra – the ultimate optimizer for graph traversal! Unlike its single-direction counterpart, Bidirectional Dijkstra employs two simultaneous searches: one starting from your origin and another from your destination. Both searches expand outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on their cumulative cost from their respective starting points. This is typically achieved using priority queues, which efficiently store and retrieve nodes with the lowest path cost, ensuring that the algorithm always explores the most promising paths first. The magic happens when these two searches "meet in the middle," effectively halving the search space and dramatically accelerating the computation of the guaranteed shortest path.

Here, we've brought this powerful algorithm to life on a real-world map of Durban's Morningside. We've transformed this urban landscape into a graph where intersections are our "nodes" and the streets connecting them are our "edges." Each edge has a weight, representing the travel time or distance. The red and green lines you see visualize the exploration process – the red lines are edges visited by the search originating from the start point, while the green lines are those explored by the search from the destination. The triumphant yellow line? That's the computed shortest path, a testament to the efficiency of Bidirectional Dijkstra in action. Notice how the complexity of a real-world road network, with its irregular junctions and varying road lengths, differs significantly from a simple grid. Bidirectional Dijkstra excels at navigating these complexities, ensuring you get to your destination with optimal efficiency.
