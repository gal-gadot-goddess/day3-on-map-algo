**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *fastest* route so quickly? This visualization dives deep into the Bidirectional Dijkstra algorithm, a powerful optimization technique for finding the shortest path in weighted graphs. Unlike a standard Dijkstra that expands outwards from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra performs *two* searches simultaneously: one forward from the starting point and another backward from the destination. These searches expand outwards, intelligently prioritizing neighbors based on their estimated distance to their respective goals, often using a **priority queue** to manage exploration. The algorithm terminates when the two search frontiers meet, guaranteeing the globally shortest path with significantly fewer node expansions and edge relaxations compared to a unidirectional approach.

Our "Real Map Implementation" brings this theoretical powerhouse to life! We've transformed a section of Warsaw, Poland (specifically the Wola district!) into a complex graph where intersections are **nodes** and streets are **edges**. Each edge is weighted by travel time or distance, reflecting real-world road networks. In this visualization, the expanding red and green lines represent the "visited edges" of our forward and backward searches, respectively, as they explore the graph. The computed shortest path is highlighted in yellow. You'll notice how the bidirectional approach can be much more efficient on real-world road networks than on simple grids, as the complexity of the graph's structure (street layouts, one-way systems) can influence how quickly the two search frontiers converge.
