**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Witness the speed king of pathfinding! Our visualization showcases the Bidirectional A* algorithm navigating the charming streets of Edinburgh's New Town. This technique is a marvel of graph theory, employing two simultaneous A* searches – one from the start point and one from the goal. Each search employs a **priority queue** (often implemented as a min-heap) to store and efficiently retrieve nodes based on their f-score, which is calculated as `f = g + h`. Here, 'g' is the actual cost from the start node to the current node, and 'h' is the heuristic estimate of the cost from the current node to the goal. By intelligently exploring neighbors with the lowest f-score, each search efficiently expands outwards, prioritizing promising paths. The magic happens when the two search frontiers meet, creating a complete path much faster than a single-direction search.

Transforming a real-world city like Edinburgh's New Town into a navigable graph involves a meticulous process. Each **intersection** becomes a **node**, and the **streets** connecting them represent the **edges**. The "visited edges" are visualized in red and green, indicating the paths explored by our forward and backward searches, respectively. The final computed path, a vibrant yellow line, highlights the optimal route found by the algorithm. Unlike traditional grid-based pathfinding, real-world road networks introduce complexities. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, and complex intersection geometries contribute to more dynamic edge weights and a less predictable search space, making Bidirectional A*'s efficiency even more pronounced.
