**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find *a* path, even if it's not always the *shortest*? Our visualization dives into the fascinating world of pathfinding algorithms, specifically **Greedy Best-First Search**. Unlike exhaustive algorithms that explore every possibility, Greedy Best-First employs a heuristic to make informed guesses. At each step, it prioritizes exploring the node that *appears* to be closest to the target, based on a predefined evaluation function. This greedy approach utilizes a **Priority Queue** to efficiently manage and select the most promising neighbor to visit next, aiming for rapid convergence towards the destination.

This video showcases a **Real Map Implementation** of this algorithm, transforming the vibrant streets of Helsinki's Kallio district into a navigable graph. Here, intersections act as our **nodes**, and the streets connecting them are the **edges**. The vibrant red and green lines trace the algorithm's exploration, highlighting which edges (streets) it has "visited" as it navigates the urban landscape. The final computed path is represented by the bright yellow line. It's crucial to note the complexity difference: while grid-based pathfinding often deals with uniform edge costs, real-world road networks present dynamic and variable edge weights (travel time, distance, traffic), making the heuristic's accuracy even more impactful, and sometimes leading to less optimal routes than algorithms like A* which consider actual cost.
