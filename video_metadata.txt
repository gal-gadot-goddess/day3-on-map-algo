## Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra 🗺️✨

Ever wondered how your navigation app finds the fastest route? Often, it's powered by algorithms like Bidirectional Dijkstra! This isn't your standard Dijkstra; it's the ultimate optimizer for shortest path finding. Instead of one search radiating outwards from the start, we launch **two simultaneous searches**: one from the origin and one from the destination. These searches "bidirectionally" explore the graph, effectively meeting in the middle.

The brilliance lies in how it prioritizes exploration. Both forward and backward searches utilize **priority queues**, meticulously ordered by their current estimated distance from their respective starting points. This greedy approach ensures we're always expanding from the "closest" unexplored nodes. When the frontiers of these two searches meet (i.e., a node is visited by both searches), we've found a potential shortest path. We then prune the search space, significantly reducing the number of nodes and edges that need to be explored compared to a single-direction Dijkstra, especially in large, sparse graphs.

This visualization showcases a **Real Map Implementation** of Bidirectional Dijkstra, transforming the streets of Copenhagen's Østerbro district into a computational graph. Here, each intersection is a **node**, and each street segment connecting them is an **edge** with a weight representing travel time or distance. The vibrant red and green lines you see are the "visited edges" – the expanding frontiers of our forward and backward Dijkstra searches respectively. The illuminating yellow line? That's the computed **shortest path** the algorithm discovered, a testament to the power of graph theory in action! You'll notice the efficiency difference compared to a grid; real-world road networks are far more complex and less uniform than explicit grids, making Bidirectional Dijkstra's optimization even more impactful.
