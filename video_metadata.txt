**Real Map Implementation of DFS** 🌳🚗🗺️

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? Our latest visualization dives deep into **Depth-First Search (DFS)**, demonstrating its core logic through a real-world application. DFS operates by prioritizing exploration down a single path as far as possible before **backtracking**. When encountering a node with multiple unvisited neighbors, it arbitrarily selects one and continues its descent. This "greedy" approach can lead it down lengthy, potentially suboptimal paths, but its strength lies in its inherent simplicity and speed for certain tasks. Technically, DFS typically leverages a **stack** (either explicitly defined or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the nodes to visit and the path taken. Unlike breadth-first search which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS plunges directly into the "depths" of the graph.

This visualization brings **Graph Theory** to life in the bustling streets of **Nairobi's Westlands**. We've transformed the urban landscape into a graph where **intersections are nodes** and the **streets connecting them are edges**. Watch as the red and green lines trace the **"visited edges"** of our DFS exploration, showing the algorithm's journey through the intricate road network. The prominent **yellow line** represents the computed path, showcasing the outcome of the DFS traversal. It's fascinating to consider how real-world road networks, with their one-way streets, varying traffic conditions, and complex interconnections, present a far more nuanced challenge than a simple grid. While an explicit grid might offer predictable traversal times, navigating real roads introduces complexities like varying edge weights (travel time, distance) that DFS, in its basic form, doesn't explicitly optimize for, often leading to longer explorations to find a path compared to algorithms that consider edge weights.
