**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A* 🇸🇬**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the fastest route in a bustling city like Singapore's Downtown Core? Today, we're visualizing the **Bidirectional A\* algorithm** – often dubbed the "speed king" of pathfinding! Unlike a standard A\* search that expands outwards from a single start point, Bidirectional A\* initiates *two* simultaneous searches: one from the start node and another from the goal node. These intelligent searches race towards each other, each employing a priority queue (often implemented using a min-heap) to explore promising neighbors first. The priority is determined by the f-score, which is the sum of the g-score (the cost from the start to the current node) and the h-score (the estimated cost from the current node to the goal). When the two search frontiers meet, a path is found. This clever strategy significantly reduces the explored search space, making it incredibly efficient for large graphs, especially those found in real-world map applications.

Our visualization brings this concept to life on a real map of Singapore's Downtown Core. Here, we've translated the city's intricate network into a **graph data structure**: each intersection is a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges**. The **red and green lines** you see dynamically drawing represent the "visited edges" as each search expands its frontier, meticulously exploring potential routes. The **bright yellow line** finally materializes as the computed optimal path, the shortest route identified by the converging searches. It's crucial to note that real-world road networks present unique challenges compared to simple grids. Factors like one-way streets, varying speed limits, traffic congestion (which can be incorporated as dynamic edge weights), and complex intersections mean the graph can be significantly larger and more intricate, making the efficiency gains of Bidirectional A\* even more pronounced.
