**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute* fastest route? Meet Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer! Unlike its single-direction counterpart, this algorithm simultaneously initiates two searches – one from your starting point and another from your destination. The magic lies in how it prioritizes neighbors: both searches expand outwards, intelligently exploring nodes with the lowest cumulative cost from *their respective* starting points. This is typically managed using priority queues (like min-heaps), ensuring we always expand the most promising frontier. When these two expanding frontiers meet, we've found our guaranteed shortest path – often dramatically faster than a single, unidirectional search, especially in large and complex graphs.

Our visualization brings this powerful concept to life on the real streets of Lyon's Presqu'île! Here, the city itself becomes our graph: each intersection is a "node," and the streets connecting them are "edges" with associated weights (travel time or distance). The red and green lines you see are the "visited edges" from our forward and backward searches, respectively, actively exploring the graph. The vibrant yellow line? That's the optimal path, computed precisely where our two searches converged. While a grid is a simple graph, real-world road networks present unique complexities. Edge weights aren't uniform, one-way streets add directional constraints, and the inherent interconnectedness can lead to vast search spaces, making Bidirectional Dijkstra's efficiency particularly impactful here.
