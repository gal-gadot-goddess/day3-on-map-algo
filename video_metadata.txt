**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how a navigation app might find its way through a maze of streets? This visualization dives into the depths of Depth-First Search (DFS), a powerful graph traversal algorithm. DFS operates by exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When it encounters a node, it prioritizes visiting one of its unvisited neighbors. If all neighbors have been visited, it backtracks to the most recent node with unvisited neighbors. This systematic exploration is typically implemented using a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the path currently being explored. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS's depth-first approach can be highly efficient for certain problems, though it's important to note that it can sometimes get "lost" down a long, unproductive path if not managed carefully.

It's crucial to recognize the nuances of real-world graphs versus simple grids: street networks often have one-way streets, varying speeds, and complex junctions, leading to different complexity considerations compared to a perfectly structured grid. DFS's speed can be a significant advantage here, quickly finding *a* path, though not necessarily the *shortest* path without modifications.
