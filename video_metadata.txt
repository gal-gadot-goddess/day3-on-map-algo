## Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First

Ever wondered how navigation apps decide the fastest route? 🤔 Let's dive into the fascinating world of graph theory and pathfinding algorithms with a real-world application!

In this visualization, we're exploring the **Greedy Best-First Search algorithm** on a map of Vancouver's Strathcona neighborhood. The core idea behind Greedy Best-First is its direct pursuit of the goal. It prioritizes exploring neighbors based on a **heuristic function** that estimates the "cost" to reach the target from that neighbor. Unlike algorithms like Dijkstra's, which consider the actual cost to reach a node, Greedy Best-First is purely "greedy" – it takes the path that *looks* most promising at each step. To achieve this, it typically utilizes a **priority queue** to store nodes to be explored. The priority queue orders nodes based on their heuristic values, ensuring that the node closest (heuristically) to the target is always dequeued and expanded first. This makes it incredibly fast for finding *a* path, but it's important to note it doesn't guarantee the *optimal* path.

Our beautiful Vancouver map is transformed into a **graph** where each **intersection** acts as a **node** and each **street segment** connecting two intersections represents an **edge**. In the visualization, the **red and green lines** signify the **visited edges** – the areas the algorithm has explored in its attempt to reach the destination. The vibrant **yellow line** traces the **computed path** found by the Greedy Best-First algorithm. Implementing pathfinding on real-world road networks presents unique challenges compared to simpler grid-based problems. Factors like varying street lengths, one-way streets, traffic conditions (not depicted here but a real-world consideration!), and complex intersection geometries add layers of complexity. The greedy approach, while efficient, might overlook a slightly longer but ultimately faster route if its heuristic misjudges the immediate neighborhood's proximity to the target.
