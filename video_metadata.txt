**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First 📍 La Paz (El Prado), Bolivia**

This visualization showcases the Greedy Best-First search algorithm navigating the vibrant streets of La Paz! 🇧🇴 Greedy Best-First is an informed search algorithm that prioritizes exploring nodes that appear to be closest to the goal. Its core logic hinges on a heuristic function, `h(n)`, which estimates the cost from a node `n` to the target. Unlike A* search, Greedy Best-First does *not* consider the cost already incurred to reach a node (`g(n)`). This means it makes decisions based purely on the immediate perceived distance to the destination. To manage its exploration, it typically utilizes a **priority queue**. When expanding a node, it adds its unvisited neighbors to the priority queue, ordered by their heuristic values. The algorithm then repeatedly extracts the node with the lowest heuristic value from the priority queue until the target node is reached. This "straight-line" approach, while often fast, can lead it down suboptimal paths.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the complex urban landscape of La Paz into a graph structure. Here, **nodes represent intersections**, the crucial points where streets converge, and **edges represent the streets connecting these intersections**. The red and green lines you see are **"visited edges"** as the algorithm explores possible routes. The vibrant **yellow line** illustrates the **computed path** from the start to the target destination. Implementing graph algorithms on real-world road networks introduces complexities absent in explicit grid-based scenarios. Road networks are often irregular, with varying edge weights (travel times, distances, one-way restrictions), and may not always form a perfectly connected or easily traversable structure. This means the performance and effectiveness of algorithms like Greedy Best-First can be significantly impacted by the intricacies of actual urban planning and traffic conditions.
