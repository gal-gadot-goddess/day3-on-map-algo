**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wonder how navigation apps find the fastest route? This visualization showcases the power of Bidirectional Dijkstra, a highly efficient algorithm for finding the shortest path in weighted graphs. Unlike standard Dijkstra's which explores outwards from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the start node and another backward from the target node. Both searches expand by prioritizing nodes with the lowest accumulated cost, typically using a **priority queue**. When the two search frontiers meet, the shortest path is definitively found. This "meeting in the middle" strategy significantly prunes the search space, making it much faster than a single-direction search, especially in dense or large graphs.

In this **Real Map Implementation**, we've transformed the city of Reykjavík, specifically the Laugardalur area, into a graph. Each intersection is a **node**, and the streets connecting them are **edges** with weights representing travel time or distance. The red and green lines you see are the **visited edges** from our forward and backward searches respectively, demonstrating how the algorithm intelligently explores the city's road network. The striking yellow line represents the final, computed shortest path between our chosen start and end points. The efficiency gains are particularly notable on real-world road networks compared to simple grid-based graphs, as road intersections are less uniform, leading to more effective pruning of search branches.
