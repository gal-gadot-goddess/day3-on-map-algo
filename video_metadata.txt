**Real Map Implementation of BFS** 📍✨

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the shortest path? Today, we're diving deep into **Breadth-First Search (BFS)** with a real-world twist! 🗺️ Our visualization showcases BFS traversing the intricate streets of Sydney's Central Business District. At its core, BFS is a graph traversal algorithm that systematically explores a graph level by level. It starts at a designated 'source' node and visits all its immediate neighbors. Then, for each of those neighbors, it visits their unvisited neighbors, and so on. This "expanding outwards" behavior is crucial: BFS inherently prioritizes exploring all nodes at a given "depth" before moving to nodes at the next depth. This ensures that the first time BFS encounters the 'target' node, it has found the shortest path in terms of the number of edges traversed.

To achieve this, BFS meticulously uses a **Queue** data structure. When a node is visited, its unvisited neighbors are enqueued. The algorithm then dequeues the next node from the front of the queue to explore, effectively processing nodes in a First-In, First-Out (FIFO) manner. This contrasts with Depth-First Search (DFS), which typically uses a Stack (or recursion) to explore as deeply as possible along one branch before backtracking.

For our "Real Map Implementation," we've transformed the bustling Sydney CBD into a graph. Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. The red and green lines you see on the map represent the **visited edges** as our BFS algorithm explores the graph. These are the streets the algorithm has processed to expand its search. The vibrant yellow line, on the other hand, signifies the **computed path** from our starting point to a chosen destination. It's important to note that while BFS finds the path with the fewest *street segments* (edges), this doesn't always directly translate to the fastest *driving time* in a real-world scenario. Factors like traffic, one-way streets, and turn restrictions (which add complexity beyond simple graph edges) can significantly impact travel duration, making direct grid-based BFS a simplified model for intricate urban navigation.
