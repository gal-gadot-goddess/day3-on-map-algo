**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra 🗺️💡**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest route so fast? We're visualizing Bidirectional Dijkstra, a sophisticated graph traversal algorithm that's the ultimate optimizer for finding the shortest path in weighted graphs. Unlike its unidirectional counterpart, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one from the source node and another from the destination node. Each search expands outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on their cumulative path cost. This is typically managed using priority queues, which efficiently store and retrieve nodes with the lowest accumulated distance, ensuring that the exploration always proceeds along the most promising paths. By meeting in the middle, these two searches drastically reduce the overall search space compared to a single search exploring all the way to the destination.

In this "Real Map Implementation," we've transformed a section of Gothenburg, specifically the Masthugget district, into a navigable graph. Each intersection is a "node," and the streets connecting them are "edges" with associated weights (representing travel time or distance). The visualization you see showcases this process: the red and green lines represent the "visited edges" as our two Dijkstra searches expand their frontiers, exploring the city's intricate street network. The moment these two expanding search frontiers meet, the algorithm has found its shortest path. The bright yellow line then highlights this computed shortest path, meticulously determined by analyzing the combined costs from both searches. It's important to note that applying Dijkstra to real-world road networks is significantly more complex than on a simple grid. Road networks are irregular, with varying edge weights and connectivity, demanding robust data structures and efficient search strategies to handle the inherent complexity.
