**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest routes or how algorithms explore complex networks? Today, we're diving deep into **Depth-First Search (DFS)**, a powerful graph traversal algorithm, visualized on the real streets of Hanoi's Ba Đình district! 🗺️

DFS operates by exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When presented with a node (an intersection in our map analogy), DFS prioritizes one unvisited neighbor and immediately explores its entire subtree. It continues this "plunge" until it hits a dead end or a fully explored path. To manage this depth-first exploration, DFS fundamentally utilizes a **stack** data structure (often implicitly through recursion, or explicitly). Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS's stack ensures that the *last* neighbor visited is the *first* one to be explored further. This strategy can sometimes lead it down long, potentially less optimal paths (it can "get lost" in the maze!), but its simplicity and speed in certain scenarios make it incredibly efficient.

Our "Real Map Implementation" brings abstract graph theory to life! We've transformed the intricate network of streets and intersections in Ba Đình, Hanoi into a graph. Each **intersection** acts as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. In this visualization:
- The **red lines** indicate streets that have been explored and are being *backtracked* from.
- The **green lines** show streets that have been *visited* and are part of the current exploration path.
- The **vibrant yellow line** highlights the final computed path found by the DFS algorithm.

It's crucial to note the complexity difference between explicit grid-based graphs and real-world road networks. While an algorithm might perform uniformly on a perfect grid, real roads have varying lengths, one-way restrictions, and varying traffic densities. Our visualization simplifies these realities, but the core DFS logic demonstrates how it navigates this complex urban graph, prioritizing depth and speed over exhaustive breadth, and showing the path it carves through the cityscape.
