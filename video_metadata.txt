**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute fastest* route in complex urban landscapes? Enter Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer! Instead of a single search radiating outwards, this algorithm cleverly initiates *two simultaneous searches* – one forward from your starting point and another backward from your destination. Each search explores its local neighborhood, prioritizing neighbors with the lowest cumulative cost (distance, time, etc.) using a **priority queue**. As these two search frontiers expand and eventually meet, the point of intersection signifies the discovery of the guaranteed shortest path. This dual-pronged approach dramatically reduces the search space compared to a unidirectional search, especially in dense graphs.

Witnessing Bidirectional Dijkstra in action on the streets of Seoul's Yeongdeungpo-gu is a tangible demonstration of Graph Theory! We've transformed the intricate road network into a graph where **intersections are nodes** and **streets are weighted edges**. The mesmerizing red and green lines you see are the **"visited edges"** – the exploration frontiers of our forward and backward Dijkstra searches, meticulously expanding through the graph. The gleaming yellow line represents the computed, optimal path found where these two explorations converged. Unlike the predictable complexity of grid-based pathfinding, real-world road networks present unique challenges: varying edge weights (traffic, one-way streets), irregular node connections, and the sheer scale of interconnectedness, making efficient algorithms like Bidirectional Dijkstra indispensable.
