**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find *a* route, even if it's not always the *most efficient*? Meet Greedy Best-First Search, our algorithm of choice for this real-world map visualization! 🌍 At its core, Greedy Best-First prioritizes exploring nodes based on a heuristic evaluation function that estimates the "cost" or "distance" from the current node directly to the goal. It doesn't look ahead at future costs (like Dijkstra's), but rather makes the locally optimal choice at each step by always expanding the node that *appears* closest to the target. For efficient neighbor selection and prioritization, this strategy typically leverages a **priority queue**. The priority queue stores nodes, ordered by their heuristic value, allowing the algorithm to quickly extract the "best" node to explore next. While it's incredibly fast and often finds a path, its greedy nature means it can sometimes get "stuck" exploring a suboptimal branch if the heuristic is misleading.

In this visualization, we've transformed a section of Oslo's Majorstuen district into a graph representation. Each **intersection** is a **node**, and each **street segment** connecting two intersections is an **edge**. The **red and green lines** you see trace the algorithm's exploration of the graph—the red lines represent edges that have been visited and evaluated, while green indicates the current frontier of exploration. The **bright yellow line** is the final path computed by Greedy Best-First from our starting point to the target. Implementing pathfinding on real road networks introduces complexities beyond simple grid-based environments. Road networks have varying edge weights (street lengths, speed limits, one-way streets), and the density and connectivity of intersections can significantly impact performance. Compared to explicit grids where movement is restricted to cardinal directions, real-world graphs are often more sparse but can have more intricate connections, making the choice of heuristic and data structures even more critical.
