**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A***

Witness the speed! Our visualization showcases the **Bidirectional A\* algorithm**, a true "speed king" for pathfinding. Unlike a standard A\* that searches from a single start point, Bidirectional A\* employs two simultaneous searches: one originating from the start node and another from the goal node. These intelligent searches "race" towards each other, significantly reducing the search space and computational cost, especially on large graphs.

At its core, Bidirectional A\* prioritizes nodes based on a cost function, typically `f(n) = g(n) + h(n)`. Here, `g(n)` represents the cost from the *originating search's* start node to the current node `n`, and `h(n)` is a heuristic estimate of the cost from node `n` to the *originating search's* goal node. For Bidirectional A\*, we actually maintain *two* cost functions and heuristic estimates, one for each search direction. Both searches typically utilize a **priority queue** to efficiently retrieve the node with the lowest `f(n)` value, exploring the most promising paths first. When the search frontiers of the two searches meet, a path is found. The algorithm terminates when the meeting point is confirmed to be the optimal path.

Transforming a real-world city like **Bucharest (Sector 6)** into a navigable graph is a fascinating application of **Graph Theory**. Each **intersection** in Sector 6 becomes a **node** in our graph, and the **streets connecting them** are the **edges**. The visualization vividly illustrates this: the **red and green lines** represent the **visited edges** of our bidirectional search – the exploration frontiers expanding from both the start and end points. The **shimmering yellow line** then highlights the shortest, computed **path** found by the algorithm once the two searches converge. Unlike explicit grids where node neighbors are predictable (up, down, left, right), real road networks present a more complex topology. Street layouts, one-way systems, and varying street lengths introduce higher branching factors and non-uniform edge weights, making Bidirectional A\*'s intelligent exploration and efficiency even more critical.
