**Real Map Implementation of DFS 🗺️🚶‍♀️💨**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? Today, we're diving deep into **Depth-First Search (DFS)**, visualized on the vibrant streets of Reykjavík's Miðborg! 🇮🇸 DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. Think of it like this: when faced with multiple paths, DFS arbitrarily chooses one and relentlessly follows it until it hits a dead end or finds its target. Then, it pops back to the last decision point and tries another unvisited branch. This "plunge deep" strategy is often implemented using a **stack** (implicitly through recursion or explicitly) to keep track of the current path and the nodes that need further exploration. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS prioritizes depth, making it incredibly efficient for finding *any* path between two nodes, though not necessarily the shortest.

This visualization brings graph theory to life by transforming the bustling city of Reykjavík (Miðborg) into a digital graph! 🏙️ Each **intersection** is a node, and the **streets connecting them** are the edges. The red and green lines you see represent the **visited edges** as our DFS algorithm explores the network. Red typically signifies an edge that has been explored and backtracked from, while green marks the current active path being investigated. The bright **yellow line** elegantly highlights the computed path found by DFS from our starting point to the destination. It's crucial to note that real-world road networks can be far more complex than simple grid graphs. Factors like one-way streets, traffic congestion, and varying road lengths introduce dynamic complexities that traditional grid-based DFS might not fully capture without additional weighting or heuristics, impacting traversal speed and path optimality.
