**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the quickest route so fast? Let's dive into Bidirectional Dijkstra's magic! Unlike a standard Dijkstra, which expands outward from a single source, this algorithm initiates *two* simultaneous searches: one from your starting point and another from your destination. Both searches maintain their own priority queues, meticulously exploring neighbors based on the accumulated edge weights (distance/travel time). The key is that each search expands *inward*, prioritizing nodes that offer the most promising paths towards the *other* search's frontier. This intelligent exploration significantly prunes the search space, dramatically reducing the number of nodes visited compared to a unidirectional search. When the two search frontiers meet, we've guaranteed the shortest path.

This visualization brings graph theory to life using a real-world map of Sapporo's Chuo-ku! We've transformed the city's intricate network into a graph where each intersection is a node and each street segment is an edge. The red and green lines you see represent the expanding "visited edges" of our two Dijkstra searches, visually showing how they intelligently explore the city's streets. The final yellow line is the computed shortest path, found when these two search frontiers converged. While grids offer predictable complexity, real-world road networks are far more complex with varying edge weights and connectivity, making bidirectional search incredibly efficient for finding optimal paths.
