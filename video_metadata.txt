**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Dive into the fascinating world of graph traversal with this real-world application of the Greedy Best-First search algorithm! 🗺️ We're navigating the charming streets of Prague's Žižkov district, transforming its intricate network into a graph where intersections are our **nodes** and streets are our **edges**. Greedy Best-First is all about making the locally optimal choice at each step. It doesn't look ahead to see if a seemingly good choice now might lead to a dead end later. Instead, it always picks the neighboring node that appears to be closest to the target destination, typically using a heuristic function. In this visualization, we're employing a **priority queue** to manage our exploration. Each node is added to the queue, prioritized by its estimated distance to the goal. The algorithm then iteratively extracts the node with the *lowest* heuristic value (i.e., the one estimated to be closest) and expands its neighbors, adding them to the queue if they haven't been visited. This "straight shot" approach makes it remarkably efficient but, as you'll see, not always the guaranteed shortest path.

This isn't your average grid-based pathfinding! Here, we've modeled the actual urban landscape of Žižkov, Prague. The **red and green lines** you see represent the **visited edges** as Greedy Best-First explores the graph, opening up different routes from our starting point. The vibrant **yellow line** illustrates the path the algorithm ultimately computed to reach the target. Unlike the predictable, uniform costs of a grid, real-world roads have varying lengths, traffic conditions (which we abstract away here for simplicity but are crucial in more advanced pathfinding!), and connectivity. This means that a "straight" line on the map might translate to a longer traversal time or distance than a seemingly more circuitous route. The Greedy Best-First algorithm's speed is a major advantage in scenarios where a "good enough" path found quickly is more valuable than the absolute shortest path, especially in dynamic environments or when computational resources are limited.
