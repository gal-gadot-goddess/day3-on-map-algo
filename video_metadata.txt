**Real Map Implementation of DFS** 🇸🇬✨

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate the complexities of the real world? This visualization showcases Depth-First Search (DFS) in action on the vibrant streets of Singapore's Marina Bay! 🌃

DFS is a powerful graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When encountering a node (an intersection, in our case), it systematically explores one of its unvisited neighbors. This process continues recursively or iteratively, creating a deep dive into a particular path. At its core, DFS typically employs a **stack** (either explicitly or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the nodes to visit. This LIFO (Last-In, First-Out) nature is what drives its "go deep first" strategy. Unlike algorithms that prioritize breadth or cost (like BFS or Dijkstra's using a Queue or Priority Queue respectively), DFS's neighbor selection is often arbitrary among unvisited options, making it efficient for finding *a* path quickly, but it can sometimes get "lost" down a long, unproductive branch before it discovers a shorter, more optimal one.

This isn't just an abstract grid; it's a **Real Map Implementation**! We've transformed Singapore's iconic Marina Bay into a graph where **nodes represent intersections** and **edges represent the streets connecting them**. The vibrant **red and green lines** you see are the "visited edges" of our DFS algorithm actively exploring the city's network. The **shimmering yellow line** represents the final computed path, tracing a route discovered by DFS. It's crucial to note that the complexity of graph traversal on real-world road networks can differ significantly from explicit grid graphs. Factors like one-way streets, varying road lengths, and the sheer density of connections in a city like Singapore introduce nuances that algorithms must handle beyond simple uniform edge costs. DFS's speed can be a significant advantage here when a quick, albeit not necessarily optimal, route is needed, especially in scenarios where the graph is very large and deep.
