**Real Map Implementation of DFS** 🗺️✨

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? Today, we're diving deep into Depth-First Search (DFS) with a real-world twist! DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When it encounters a node with multiple unvisited neighbors, it arbitrarily chooses one and plunges in. This "go deep first" strategy often utilizes a **Stack** (explicitly or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the path. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a Queue to explore level by level, DFS prioritizes depth, making it efficient for finding a specific target or exploring all reachable nodes in a connected component. While it might sometimes "get lost" by going down a very long, unproductive path, its inherent simplicity and often faster performance for certain tasks make it a valuable tool in our algorithmic toolkit.

This visualization brings DFS to life on the streets of Zürich's Seefeld district! We've transformed this familiar urban landscape into a graph where intersections are **nodes** and streets are **edges**. The red and green lines you see illustrate the DFS traversal itself – the red marking edges that have been explored and are being backtracked from, and the green showing the currently active exploration path. The bright yellow line represents the final computed path found by our DFS. Implementing DFS on real-world road networks presents unique challenges compared to simple grid-based graphs. Factors like varying road lengths, one-way streets, and traffic congestion can significantly impact traversal speed and the definition of an "optimal" path, making explicit grid traversals seem almost trivial in comparison.
