**Real Map Implementation of DFS** 🗺️

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate the complexities of our world? Here, we visualize Depth-First Search (DFS) bringing the ancient alleys of Kathmandu's Boudha to life! 🕉️ DFS is a graph traversal algorithm that explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. At its core, DFS prioritizes diving deep into one neighbor's path until it hits a dead end or has explored all possibilities within that branch. It fundamentally operates using a **stack** (either explicitly managed or implicitly through recursion) to keep track of the nodes to visit. Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue and explores level by level, DFS's inherent recursive or stack-based nature means it's always aiming for the deepest possible node before reconsidering its options. This can lead to it finding a path quickly if one exists, but also to potentially getting lost in very deep branches if not managed carefully.

Our "Real Map Implementation" transforms the intricate network of Kathmandu (Boudha) into a graph. Each **intersection** serves as a **node**, and the **streets connecting them are the edges**. In this visualization, the **red and green lines** represent the **visited edges** as DFS actively explores the graph, trying to find a path from a starting point to a destination. The vibrant **yellow line** then highlights the **computed path** that DFS successfully identified. It's fascinating to see how graph theory applies to real-world scenarios! While an explicit grid might offer predictable branching, real-world road networks like Boudha's can have highly irregular connectivity, with dead ends and complex intersections, presenting unique challenges and opportunities for pathfinding algorithms. The time complexity of DFS on an explicit grid is typically O(V + E), where V is the number of vertices (intersections) and E is the number of edges (streets). However, on a real-world graph, the actual traversal time can be significantly influenced by the graph's structure and the efficiency of the underlying data representation.
