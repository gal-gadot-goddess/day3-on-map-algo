**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute* fastest route? This visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer for finding the guaranteed shortest path in weighted graphs. Unlike a standard Dijkstra that expands outwards from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra initiates two simultaneous searches: one forward from the origin and one backward from the destination. These searches expand their frontiers, typically using priority queues to always explore the lowest-cost edge from their respective starting points. The key to its efficiency lies in meeting in the "middle" – when the two search frontiers intersect, we've found our guaranteed shortest path. This duality significantly reduces the search space compared to a unidirectional approach, especially in large graphs.

Our implementation brings this powerful algorithm to life on a real-world map of Edinburgh's New Town! Here, we've translated the cityscape into a graph: each intersection becomes a 'node', and the streets connecting them are 'edges'. The weights on these edges represent the travel time or distance along those streets. In the visualization, the expanding red and green lines depict the edges being explored by the forward and backward searches, respectively. Notice how they efficiently prune the search space by "feeling" their way towards each other. The bright yellow line then beautifully highlights the computed shortest path, demonstrating the seamless convergence of our two intelligent searches. While grids are a common way to visualize pathfinding, real-world road networks introduce complexities like one-way streets and varying speed limits, making bidirectional approaches particularly advantageous.
