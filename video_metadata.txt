**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how your navigation app finds the *absolute* fastest route through a bustling city? It's not magic, it's sophisticated graph theory! Our visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer for finding shortest paths in weighted graphs. Unlike a standard Dijkstra that expands outward from a single source, Bidirectional Dijkstra cleverly launches *two* searches simultaneously: one from the origin (Source) and another from the destination (Target). These searches expand in parallel, prioritizing neighbors based on their current shortest known distance from their respective starting points. This is typically managed using priority queues (min-heaps) which store nodes and their associated costs, ensuring we always explore the most promising paths first. The algorithm terminates when the two search frontiers meet, guaranteeing the shortest path with significantly reduced computational effort.

Implementing Dijkstra on real-world road networks, which are often irregular and complex, presents unique challenges compared to simple grid-based graphs.
