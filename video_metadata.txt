**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional A\***

Ever wondered how your GPS finds the fastest route in a blink? Meet Bidirectional A*, the speed king of pathfinding algorithms. Instead of a single search radiating outwards, Bidirectional A* employs two intelligent searches simultaneously: one starting from your origin and another from your destination. Both searches expand outwards, prioritizing neighbors based on their estimated total cost (g-score + h-score). The g-score is the actual cost from the start/end node to the current node, while the h-score is the heuristic estimate of the cost from the current node to the goal. The algorithm uses two priority queues, one for each search direction, to efficiently select the most promising nodes to explore next. When the two search frontiers meet, a path is found, significantly reducing the search space compared to a standard A* search.

Our implementation transforms the intricate street network of Porto's Foz do Douro into a graph. Each intersection becomes a node, and the streets connecting them represent edges. The cost of traversing an edge is determined by the real-world travel time (or distance) between those intersections. In this visualization, the red and green lines trace the "visited edges" of our two searches, showcasing how efficiently they explore the graph. The yellow line elegantly illustrates the final computed path, demonstrating the successful convergence of both search directions. While Bidirectional A* excels on explicit grids, its application to real-world road networks presents unique challenges and complexities due to irregular node connectivity and variable edge weights.
