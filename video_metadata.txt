**Real Map Implementation of Bidirectional Dijkstra**

Ever wondered how navigation apps find the *absolute* fastest route? Meet Bidirectional Dijkstra, the ultimate optimizer! Unlike its single-direction counterpart, this algorithm launches two simultaneous searches: one from your starting point and another from your destination. Each search expands outwards, intelligently prioritizing neighbors based on their cumulative edge weights (distance, in this case) using a **priority queue**. This data structure ensures that we always explore the "closest" nodes first, minimizing the overall search space. When the two search frontiers meet, we've definitively found the guaranteed shortest path – and often, much faster than a single search would!

This visualization showcases Bidirectional Dijkstra in action on a real-world map of Valparaíso's vibrant Cerro Alegre. We've transformed this charming cityscape into a graph: **nodes** represent street intersections, and **edges** are the streets connecting them, each assigned a weight (likely distance or estimated travel time). The **red and green lines** you see are the "visited edges" – the graph exploration by our forward and backward searches, respectively. The bright **yellow line** is the computed shortest path, elegantly connecting your start and end points. While grids are often used for simpler Dijkstra demonstrations, real-world road networks present unique challenges: irregular shapes, varying edge weights, and potentially disconnected components, making optimized algorithms like Bidirectional Dijkstra essential for efficient navigation.
