**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First 🗺️🚀**

Ever wondered how navigation apps might *quickly* find a path? Meet Greedy Best-First Search! This algorithm is designed for speed, making decisions based on a heuristic that estimates the "cost" to reach the target from each neighbor. Unlike Uniform Cost Search or A*, it doesn't consider the cost already accumulated to *reach* the current node. It purely prioritizes neighbors that *appear* closest to the goal. In our implementation, we use a **priority queue** to efficiently manage these neighbors. The priority is determined by our heuristic function (in this case, Euclidean distance to the target), always pulling out the neighbor with the *lowest* estimated cost to the destination. This "greedy" nature allows it to make rapid progress, often finding a path to the target with remarkable speed.

This visualization brings the abstract concept of graph theory to life on the actual streets of Porto's historic Miragaia district! Each intersection is a **node**, and the connecting streets are **edges**. The red and green lines you see represent the edges being "visited" as the algorithm explores outward from our starting point. The algorithm prioritizes expanding through edges that it *believes* will lead it closer to the destination. The continuous yellow line showcases the computed path found by Greedy Best-First. It's crucial to note the difference between explicit grid-based graphs and real-world road networks. While Greedy Best-First excels at quickly finding *a* path in complex, real-world scenarios, its greedy nature means it might not always find the *absolute shortest* or *optimal* path due to the intricacies and potential dead ends of a real road system compared to a simplified grid.
