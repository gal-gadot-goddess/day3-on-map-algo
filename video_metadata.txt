**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First**

Ever wondered how navigation apps *really* work under the hood? In this visualization, we're putting the **Greedy Best-First search algorithm** to the test on a real-world map of Glasgow's vibrant Merchant City! 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Greedy Best-First is an informed search algorithm that prioritizes exploring nodes based on an evaluation function, aiming to reach the goal as quickly as possible. Unlike Dijkstra's, which guarantees the shortest path, Greedy Best-First uses a heuristic function to estimate the cost from the current node to the target. It greedily expands the most "promising" node (the one closest to the target, according to the heuristic) first. Technically, it uses a **priority queue** to manage nodes to be explored, ordered by their heuristic values. This means it doesn't look ahead at the overall path cost, but rather makes decisions based purely on the estimated distance to the goal at each step.

Our implementation transforms the intricate streets of Merchant City into a **graph**, where intersections are **nodes** and the roads connecting them are **edges**. The red and green lines you see trace the algorithm's exploration of the graph – the red lines represent edges that have been explored and added to the priority queue, while the green lines indicate edges that have been fully processed. The pulsating yellow line showcases the path computed by Greedy Best-First from our starting point to the target destination. Notice how the algorithm zigs and zags, attempting to move directly towards the goal. This direct approach makes it incredibly fast, often outperforming algorithms that explore more exhaustively. However, on complex real-world road networks, this can sometimes lead to suboptimal routes compared to algorithms that consider total path cost, as it might commit to a seemingly direct but ultimately longer path.
