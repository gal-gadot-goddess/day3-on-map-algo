**Real Map Implementation of Greedy Best-First** 🗺️📍

Ever wondered how navigation apps *think*? Let's dive into the Greedy Best-First search algorithm in action on the streets of Oslo! Unlike A*, which balances cost-so-far with estimated cost-to-go, Greedy Best-First is purely heuristic-driven. At each step, it greedily expands the node that appears to be closest to the target, as estimated by its heuristic function (in this case, likely the straight-line or Euclidean distance). It uses a **priority queue** to efficiently manage and retrieve the most promising node to explore next. This makes it incredibly swift as it relentlessly pursues the goal, but be warned: this tunnel vision means it might miss shorter or more optimal paths if they involve a brief detour.

In this visualization, we've transformed the intricate road network of Oslo's Frogner district into a graph structure. Each intersection is a **node**, and each street segment connecting them is an **edge**. The red and green lines you see represent **visited edges** as the algorithm explores the graph, dynamically expanding its search. The bright yellow line traces the **computed path** from start to finish. Implementing this on a real-world map presents unique challenges compared to a simple grid. Road networks have varying edge weights (travel times, distances), one-way streets, and complex interconnections, making the heuristic's accuracy and the algorithm's tendency to find *a* path versus the *optimal* path even more pronounced.
