**Real Map Implementation of DFS**

Ever wondered how algorithms navigate complex networks? In this visualization, we're diving deep into **Depth-First Search (DFS)**, a fundamental graph traversal algorithm. DFS operates by exploring as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. When faced with multiple unvisited neighbors, it arbitrarily picks one and dives in. This sequential exploration is typically managed using a **stack** (either explicitly defined or implicitly through recursion). Unlike Breadth-First Search (BFS) which uses a queue to explore level by level, DFS's "go deep first" approach makes it efficient for finding a path in a large, interconnected structure, though it can sometimes get "lost" down a very long, unproductive path.

Our "real map implementation" brings graph theory to life in the vibrant streets of Istanbul's Karaköy district! We've transformed the city's intricate network into a graph where intersections represent **nodes** and the streets connecting them are **edges**. The red and green lines you see illustrate the algorithm's exploration process – **red** highlights an explored edge, while **green** indicates a **visited edge** as DFS deepens its search. The ultimate **yellow line** represents the computed path found by DFS. It's important to note that the complexity of applying graph algorithms on real-world road networks differs from explicit grid-based graphs. Factors like one-way streets, varying traffic conditions, and road closures introduce complexities that simple grid representations don't capture, making the real-world application of DFS fascinating and challenging.
